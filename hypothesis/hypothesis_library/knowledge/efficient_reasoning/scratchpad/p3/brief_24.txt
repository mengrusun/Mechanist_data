Closest prior work directly covers this space: counterfactual-world reasoning benchmarks ("Flying Pigs, FaR and Beyond", arXiv 2505.22318), knowledge-conflict reconciliation in counterfactual reasoning (arXiv 2506.15732), and belief-bias / "failure of belief inhibition" findings that LLMs cannot suppress parametric knowledge that contradicts given premises (~14% accuracy drop; "slow thinking" only partially inhibits priors). So the phenomenon (belief bias / premise rejection) is established; the DELTA the proposal must own is: (1) framing it as a COMPUTE-INFLATION effect (more thinking tokens on belief-contradicting vs belief-neutral premises of identical logical structure), and (2) a decodable, competing internal "parametric-knowledge vs instructed-premise" signal whose strength predicts BOTH the extra compute AND the downstream error rate, with causal steering. Who cares: reasoning-robustness, hypothetical/counterfactual reasoning, and interpretability communities. Likely landing: solid if the matched-logical-structure control is clean and the competing-signal decoding is convincing; novelty is moderate because belief bias in LLMs is already documented, so the contribution rests on the compute angle plus the mechanistic tug-of-war signal rather than the phenomenon itself.
