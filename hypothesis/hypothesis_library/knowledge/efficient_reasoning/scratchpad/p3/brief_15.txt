Closest prior work: "On Reasoning Strength Planning in Large Reasoning Models" (2506.08390, NeurIPS 2025) is the direct anchor — it shows LRMs pre-plan reasoning length via a pre-allocated directional vector whose magnitude sets token count, decodable by a linear probe from question activations, and steerable (add/subtract to lengthen/shorten). "When More is Less" / optimal-length work and ThinkEdit/length-steering also treat length as a function of the CURRENT problem's own difficulty. All of this is SINGLE-problem: length is a function of the problem in isolation.
Actual delta: The proposal's novel claim is CROSS-problem CONTAMINATION ("length contagion"): the current problem's thinking budget is contaminated by the reasoning length of the immediately-preceding problem(s) in context — overthink an easy problem after a hard predecessor, underthink a hard one after an easy predecessor — carried causally by a persistent length-budget representation in the prompt-boundary residual stream that carries over from the previous problem, dissociable from the current problem's true difficulty, and steerable to restore difficulty-appropriate length. No cited prior work tests carryover of the pre-allocated length vector ACROSS problems; the "context matters / attention-budget competition" literature (Prospective Memory, distractor effects) is adjacent but about capacity exhaustion, not budget-anchoring contagion. This cross-problem framing of the known reasoning-strength-planning vector is the genuine delta.
Who cares: efficient-reasoning, batched/multi-turn inference, benchmark-design (order effects / contamination of eval), adaptive-compute. Strong "so what": if length is anchored by neighbors, per-problem compute is miscalibrated by context order, benchmark scores may have order-dependent artifacts, and a training-free steering fix restores calibration. Risk: the effect could be small or confounded with generic in-context priming; isolating "length contagion" from the previous problem's content/difficulty spillover is the crux. Would land at a strong ML/interpretability venue if the causal carryover vector is demonstrated cleanly.
