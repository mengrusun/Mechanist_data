Closest prior work: the Expected-Value-of-Control (EVC) theory from cognitive neuroscience (Shenhav/Frömer: expected reward and efficacy jointly guide cognitive-control/effort allocation). Directly relevant recent AI work: "Artificial Effort" (arXiv 2605.23920) and studies checking whether LLMs are sensitive to verbal monetary-incentive offers and whether behavior aligns with humans; plus expected-reward-prediction work for model routing. Prior AI incentive-framing work mostly looks at accuracy/behavioral compliance and simple tip prompts. The DELTA: a structured transfer of EVC's THREE separable components -- reward magnitude, effort EFFICACY, and opportunity COST of time -- to modulate reasoning COMPUTE (thinking tokens), dissociated from difficulty, carried by an internal "value" representation read at the prompt boundary, with causal steering. Who cares: efficient-reasoning (controllable test-time compute via framing), AI alignment (reward-sensitivity/incentive-gaming), and cognitive-science-to-LLM transfer. Likely landing: moderate novelty -- incentive-sensitivity of LLMs is increasingly studied ("Artificial Effort" is close), so the contribution rests on the multi-component EVC decomposition (efficacy and opportunity-cost, not just reward) plus the internal value-signal mechanism and steering, rather than the basic "incentives change effort" finding.
