Closest prior work: the human cognitive-offloading literature (delegating memory/calculation to external aids), tool-augmented reasoning, and "cognitive debt"/"confidence without competence" studies on humans using LLMs. On the model side, token-budget-aware and effort-control work shows reasoning length is malleable. The gap: no AI work shows that MERELY TELLING a model a tool is available (calculator/code interpreter) that is NEVER actually invoked reduces its internal reasoning effort (fewer thinking tokens, shallower verification, lower accuracy) on matched-content problems, dissociated from real outsourced computation, with a steerable offloading/effort-budget representation. Who cares: agent/tool-use system designers (this would be a hidden failure mode: advertising tools silently degrades reasoning), efficient-reasoning, and safety/interpretability. Likely landing: a novel and somewhat surprising behavioral phenomenon if robust. Main risks: the effect could be rational anticipation (model correctly expects to offload) rather than a bias, could be small, and "phantom" (tool announced but never used) requires careful enforcement that the tool truly is never invoked; the accuracy-drop claim needs matched-difficulty controls.
