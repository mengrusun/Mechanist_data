Closest prior work is agent/planning literature: task decomposition into subgoals, DAG-structured plans with explicit dependencies, and dependency-scheduling frameworks that prompt an LLM to judge pairwise subtask dependencies and build a dependency graph (depth-based parallel scheduling). But that work operates at the MULTI-CALL AGENT-ORCHESTRATION level (external planner arranges tool calls), not inside a single chain-of-thought. The proposal's DELTA: (1) a within-trace claim that reasoning models schedule subgoals MYOPICALLY rather than dependency-respectingly, attacking subgoals before prerequisites are established and wasting tokens on flailing/re-derivation; (2) a decodable internal "prerequisite-readiness" signal present at each subgoal onset that the model FAILS to consult; (3) steering generation toward dependency-respecting order cuts tokens without hurting accuracy. This is a fresh mechanistic framing of overthinking as a scheduling/ordering failure rather than external planning. Who cares: efficient-reasoning, reasoning-interpretability, and planning communities. Likely landing: reasonably novel because it locates the failure inside the CoT and ties it to a readiness representation; main risks are operationalizing the subgoal dependency DAG and the "readiness" ground truth cleanly, and showing that reordering (not just extra planning) is what saves tokens.
