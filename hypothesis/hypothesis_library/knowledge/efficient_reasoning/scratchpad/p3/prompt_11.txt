=== SEARCH SUMMARY ===
Closest prior work: The Illusion of Thinking (2506.06941, NeurIPS 2025) documents the exact phenomenon — reasoning effort rises with complexity then declines near a full accuracy collapse despite adequate token budget — but offers no mechanism. Temporal Predictors of Outcome (2511.14773) and Chain-of-Probe-style work show linear probes read solution quality / eventual correctness within the first few CoT tokens, i.e. early internal self-assessment exists and is linearly decodable. "Knowing When to Quit: Diagnosing and Training LLMs to Abort Futile Reasoning" (2607.29211) and "Stop Spinning Wheels" (2508.17627) treat premature/futile-reasoning detection as a FEATURE to exploit (abort earlier), the opposite framing.
Actual delta: The proposal's contribution is (a) naming a specific internal, linearly-decodable "futility" signal that PRECEDES genuine capability exhaustion and CAUSES premature effort-withdrawal (a giving-up-too-early failure), and (b) the causal counterfactual: suppressing/ablating this signal should RESTORE effort and partially recover accuracy in the collapse regime. This is the mechanistic explanation Illusion-of-Thinking lacked, and it flips the futility-detection literature (which aborts) into a suppression-to-recover intervention. Novelty risk: distinguishing "premature giving up" from "genuine incapacity" is hard; if the model is truly incapable, restoring effort won't restore accuracy, and the collapse may be real capacity limits (a live debate: Illusion vs Not-an-Illusion 2025).
Who cares: reasoning-model researchers, the very active "Illusion of Thinking" debate, efficient/adaptive-compute community, and anyone deploying reasoning models on hard tasks. A clean causal mechanism + partial recovery would be a notable contribution to that debate and could land at a strong ML venue if the accuracy restoration is real and not just more tokens.
