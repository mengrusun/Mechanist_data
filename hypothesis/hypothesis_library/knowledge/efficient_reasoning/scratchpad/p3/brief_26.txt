Closest prior work: the human Einstellung/mental-set literature, and -- importantly -- LLM-specific studies already exist: "Is your LLM trapped in a Mental Set?" (investigative study showing mental sets affect LLM reasoning) and a Nature Scientific Reports paper on LLM clinical-reasoning failures from inflexible reasoning / mARC-QA that explicitly exploits the Einstellung effect. So the phenomenon (LLMs fixate on a primed method and fail to find shortcuts) is already documented, mostly on ACCURACY/flexibility. The DELTA the proposal must own: (1) framing it as a COMPUTE/overthinking effect (extra thinking tokens spent reapplying the costly method even when solved cheaply in isolation), and (2) locating a decodable "method-commitment" representation carried over from preceding in-context problems, with causal steering to release the fixation. Who cares: efficient-reasoning, in-context-learning, and reasoning-robustness communities. Likely landing: moderate novelty -- the behavioral effect is partly known, so the contribution rests on the token-cost angle and the mechanistic method-commitment signal rather than the phenomenon; needs a clean isolated-vs-primed control showing the SAME problem is solved cheaply alone but expensively after priming.
