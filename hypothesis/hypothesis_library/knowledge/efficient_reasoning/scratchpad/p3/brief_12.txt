Closest prior work: Multiple recent papers show reasoning length is encoded pre-generation and steerable: "On Reasoning Strength Planning" / reasoning-strength directional vector, "How Far Ahead Do LLMs Plan" (2602.02103), Budget Guidance (2506.13752) which reads hidden states to predict remaining length, ThinkEdit (2503.22048), and length-direction steering (ShorterBetter, Elastic Reasoning). Probing hidden states to decode planned length from the first token / prompt boundary is established. Verbosity-compensation and RLHF-length-bias work (Verbosity != Veracity 2411.07858) shows length is partly driven by non-difficulty factors.
Actual delta: The novel angle is the CLAIM that the prompt-final (pre-generation) budget prior is substantially driven by SUPERFICIAL prompt features (phrasing, verbosity cues, presence of numbers) rather than true difficulty, and that this is SELF-FULFILLING and mis-set — i.e., a chunk of overthinking/underthinking is a mis-calibrated pre-commitment, not a reasoning need. Prior work treats the pre-generation length signal as tracking genuine difficulty and length as an emergent consequence of reasoning done; this reframes it as a causally-set, superficially-driven prior that can be steered to change length largely independently of accuracy. That reframing (superficial-feature causation + accuracy-independence) is the delta; the pre-generation-length-representation and steering mechanisms already exist.
Who cares: efficient-reasoning community, prompt-engineering/robustness researchers, adaptive-compute. Strong "so what": if length is set by surface cues before reasoning, prompts can be adversarially/benignly manipulated to cut compute, and difficulty-calibration of budgets is broken. Risk: disentangling "superficial features" from difficulty is confounded (verbose prompts may correlate with genuinely harder problems); the accuracy-independence claim is the crux and hardest to prove.
