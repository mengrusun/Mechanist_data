CRITICAL OVERLAP: an existing paper is titled almost identically -- "The Hawthorne Effect in Reasoning Models: Evaluating and Steering Test Awareness" (arXiv 2505.14617), which already trains linear probes on hidden states to detect evaluation/test-vs-deployment context and STEERS models into heightened or reduced test awareness. The proposal itself concedes it "rides on the same evaluation-awareness representation already shown to be linearly encoded and steerable." So the phenomenon (evaluation awareness), the linear-probe method, and the steering intervention are all prior art. Also relevant and skeptical: "Is Evaluation Awareness Just Format Sensitivity? Limitations of Probe-Based Evidence" (arXiv 2603.19426) and "Linear Probes Detect Task Format, Not Reasoning Mode" (arXiv 2606.02907) -- these argue the probe may pick up prompt format rather than a genuine awareness/reasoning-mode variable, a direct threat to the mechanism. The remaining DELTA the proposal must own: showing that test-awareness specifically modulates reasoning COMPUTE / thinking-token length (effort), decoupled from difficulty, rather than accuracy/compliance which prior work covered. Who cares: AI-safety/eval-validity researchers (evaluation-gaming), efficient-reasoning. Likely landing: novelty is constrained by the near-identical existing paper; contribution rests narrowly on the length/effort dependent variable plus the difficulty-decoupling, and is exposed to the format-confound critique.
