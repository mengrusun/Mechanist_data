Closest prior work: Paraphrase-consistency benchmarks (PARAREL/ParaRel, KnowledgeConsistency) long ago documented that the same fact is answered inconsistently across paraphrases/formats — this is a well-known phenomenon, so the observation itself is not new. Distributed/redundant-retrieval work (2026) shows facts are stored non-contiguously and redundantly, which partly overlaps the "multiple copies" intuition. The most direct methodological analogue is cross-lingual factual-consistency work ("Inference-Time Steering for Cross-Lingual Factual Consistency", "Paths Not Taken", "Lost in Multilinguality"), which already learns/applies steering vectors to bridge factual recall from a strong condition (English) to a weak one (other languages) and does causal tracing of where the pipeline diverges. The proposal's delta is to (a) reframe the phenomenon structurally as "many format-bound copies of one fact" rather than "one store, many query pathways" — a specific, testable structural claim — and (b) apply the causal-dissociation + steering-bridge toolkit across QUERY FORMATS (cloze/question/comparative/procedural) rather than across languages. The structural claim (redundant per-format copies that are causally dissociable, each a distinct linear direction) is the genuinely novel and non-trivial part; the format-bridge steering vector is a near-transplant of cross-lingual steering methodology. Who cares: interpretability, knowledge-editing, robustness/consistency communities. Risk: the "distinct linear direction per format" may be hard to separate from query-encoding differences (the "one store, many queries" null), which prior multi-hop work already notes (query reps diverge while fact reps stay similar) — this is the main confound that could collapse the novelty.
