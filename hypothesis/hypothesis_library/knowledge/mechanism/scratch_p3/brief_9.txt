Closest prior work: The ripple-effect editing literature is well established — RippleEdits (2023), MQuAKE (2023), ReCoE, ChainEdit/RippleCoT/PropMEND (2024-25) benchmark and try to fix propagation failures. The single most direct baseline is GradSim ("Why Does New Knowledge Create Messy Ripple Effects in LLMs?", 2024), which predicts ripple success from the cosine similarity of the GRADIENTS of the original fact and its related knowledge — Pearson up to ~0.85 across models/methods — but REQUIRES an edit (and target) to be specified and is gradient/edit-conditioned. The proposal's key differentiator: an OBSERVATIONAL, EDIT-FREE static representational-geometry coupling measure — for an entailed pair (A,B), quantify subspace alignment between the residual-stream directions that read out each answer (via linear relational embeddings + CCA / subspace angle) — with no backprop, no edit, no target answer, claimed to predict per-pair ripple success (ROME/MEMIT/in-context) with higher AUROC than GradSim and to generalize across editing methods. The relevant tooling exists: CCA-based "shared logical subspace" discovery (2026) and logical-consistency measurement work. The novel pieces are (1) reframing ripple predictability as a PRE-EXISTING, edit-independent property of how the model already stores the two facts ("consistency seams" = misaligned subspaces), (2) the static edit-free predictor beating the gradient-based GradSim without computing any edit, and (3) the causal claim that failures concentrate at seams plus a subspace-alignment intervention, and that seam pairs already show native belief inconsistency even unedited. This is a clean, well-differentiated delta over GradSim rather than a re-description. Who cares: knowledge-editing / model-updating (a large, active, high-visibility subfield), interpretability (knowledge geometry/consistency), reliability. Main risk: the edit-free coupling measure may correlate strongly with GradSim (both reflect shared storage geometry), so the "beats GradSim" and "edit-free" advantages must be demonstrated cleanly; and LRE/CCA subspace estimation is noisy and only applies to linearly-decodable relations, limiting coverage.
