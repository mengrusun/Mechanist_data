Closest prior work: A large, active multi-hop-reasoning line documents that LLMs solve derivable facts either via a memorized "shortcut" (direct entity binding) or a compositional/latent two-hop path with a decodable bridge entity — e.g., "Do LLMs Latently Perform Multi-Hop Reasoning?" (2024), "Hopping Too Late" (2024), "Understanding and Patching Compositional Reasoning" (2024, which patches the bridge/back-patches to fix two-hop errors), "How Do LLMs Compose Functions?" (2025, mechanism choice weakly correlated with accuracy, tracks embedding linearity), "Co-occurrence is not Factual Association" (2024, shortcut vs true association separable by layer), and the "curse of two-hop reasoning" generalization work (2025-26). "Factual Retrieval is Redundant/Distributed" (2026) explicitly says models have multiple functionally-equivalent paths for the same fact. So the existence of two routes, the decodable bridge entity, and even bridge-boosting interventions are established. The proposal's delta: frame route selection as an explicit per-query ARBITER, claim (1) which route drives the output is linearly decodable BEFORE the answer token, (2) "misarbitration" (committing to the weaker route while the other route internally holds the correct answer) is a substantial, identifiable failure fraction, and (3) a low-rank steering vector biasing the arbiter toward the viable route recovers answers more selectively than ROME/MEMIT. The genuinely novel piece is the arbitration/misarbitration framing as a recoverable failure mode and the pre-answer route-decodability + steer-the-arbiter recovery; the two-route existence and bridge-boosting are largely known. Who cares: interpretability, reasoning/compositionality, knowledge-editing, reliability communities. Main risk: prior bridge-patching work ("Understanding and Patching Compositional Reasoning", latent-recall boosting) already does route-biasing interventions, so the intervention delta may be thin; and "arbiter" may be a re-description of existing weak-correlation-of-mechanism-choice findings unless a distinct causal arbiter signal is isolated.
