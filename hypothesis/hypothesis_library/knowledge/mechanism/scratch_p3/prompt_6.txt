(2025, arXiv 2510.18871) establishes "guess-then-refine": early layers propose high-frequency statistical guesses later refined, and the FIRST token of a multi-token fact needs more depth than later tokens — directly the substrate for "premature lexicalization." "From Tokens to Words: Inner Lexicon" (2024) and "Token Erasure as a Footprint of Implicit Vocabulary Items" (2024) show LLMs assemble token-agnostic word/entity representations then detokenize/lexicalize — i.e., a concept-then-lexeme pipeline. Geva et al.'s subject-enrichment then attribute-extraction stages, and "Future Lens" (anticipating later tokens from one hidden state), also exist. Concept-vs-language separation is well studied for MULTILINGUAL models (language-agnostic concept space) but, as the proposal notes, not framed as concept-vs-lexeme WITHIN one language for factual error. So the two-stage concept/lexicalization structure and early high-frequency commitment are established; the proposal does not discover them. Its delta: (1) name "premature lexicalization" as a concrete factual FAILURE MODE — "right concept, wrong word": correct entity decodable as a token-agnostic concept but the output is a first-token-SHARING lexically-adjacent distractor, concentrated where the correct entity's first token is rare/high-perplexity and a distractor shares a common first token (above frequency-matched baselines); (2) dissociate a concept probe (entity via aliases/paraphrastic descriptions) from a lexical probe (committed first output token) with different layer onsets; (3) suppress the prematurely-committed distractor first-token direction (or re-score the first token conditioned on the concept) to recover the correct entity more selectively than ROME. The genuinely novel piece is the specific error-mode characterization (first-token-sharing distractor beats a correctly-selected concept) and the first-token-direction-suppression fix; the concept/lexeme two-stage geometry and guess-then-refine are borrowed. Who cares: interpretability (tokenization/detokenization, factual recall), decoding/reliability, knowledge-editing communities. Main risk: "right-concept-wrong-word" errors may be rare and hard to separate from ordinary wrong-entity errors or tokenizer artifacts; the concept probe (via aliases) may leak lexical info, and the fix may reduce to known contrastive/first-token re-ranking decoding tricks rather than a distinct mechanism.
