Closest prior work: "Inside-Out: Hidden Factual Knowledge in LLMs" (2025) established the internal-vs-external knowledge gap (~40% relative), showing probes recover facts the model never generates in 1000 samples — but it is descriptive, not mechanistic, and offers no causal routing account or intervention. ROME/Knowledge Neurons localize facts to mid-layer MLPs; Hase et al. 2023 show localization does not predict editability. Mechanistic work (e.g., factual-recall circuits, "Do I Know This Entity?" 2024) shows early MLPs write attribute info and a small number of attention heads move/select it to the last token, and activation patching is the standard causal tool. So the ingredients (hidden-knowledge gap; attention heads move facts; patching) all exist separately. The proposal's delta is to unify them into a specific causal claim — a compact, query-conditioned set of "gate" attention heads whose failure (not storage failure) explains the hidden-knowledge gap — plus a non-destructive attention intervention that reopens hidden facts and beats ROME on recovery-per-collateral. Who cares: interpretability researchers, knowledge-editing community, hallucination/reliability practitioners. Risk of overlap: the "attention heads route facts" finding is partly known; novelty rides on the gating framing, the compact transferable gate-head set, and the reopening intervention being causal and selective. This is a plausible, well-scoped mechanistic contribution rather than a wholly new phenomenon.
