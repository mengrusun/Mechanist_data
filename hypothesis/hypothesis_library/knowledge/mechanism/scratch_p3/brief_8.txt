Closest prior work: The temporal-knowledge machinery is well developed and recent. "Set the Clock" (2024) established that LLMs default to a stale internal "current year" (~2019 for LLaMA2) despite later cutoffs, and that finetuning/prompting can realign them. "Temporal Heads" (ACL 2025, arXiv 2502.14258) identifies attention heads that recall time-specific facts, disabling which degrades temporal recall while preserving general ability. Gurnee & Tegmark (2023) show linear space/time directions; "Fresh in Memory" (2025) linearly encodes training-order recency. Critically, "Temporal Alignment of Time-Sensitive Facts with Activation Engineering" (arXiv 2505.14158, 2025), Time Vectors (2023), and TARDIS (2025) already STEER temporal misalignment via activation engineering / steering vectors — though largely at the distributional/domain level. So the default-time anchor, linear time directions, temporal heads, and steering-for-temporal-alignment all exist; the proposal does not discover them. Its delta: (1) frame temporal-recall failure as an explicit CAUSAL COMPETITION between a query-INDEPENDENT default-time anchor direction and the query-SPECIFIED time direction, and show the two are separable, non-redundant directions; (2) show their magnitude IMBALANCE predicts WHICH specific facts fail (override concentrates when query year is far from the anchor and the correct answer is low-frequency, wrong answer disproportionately the default-era value); (3) demonstrate that SUPPRESSING the anchor direction (rather than editing the fact via ROME/MEMIT or globally realigning the model) selectively recovers specific stored-but-suppressed time-correct facts, with storage verified independently. The novel pieces are the anchor-vs-query-time competition mechanism, the imbalance-predicts-override diagnostic, and anchor-suppression as a targeted per-fact recovery contrasted with global realignment and fact editing; the underlying directions/heads/steering are borrowed. Who cares: temporal-QA / knowledge-updating, interpretability, retrieval/reliability, knowledge-editing communities — temporal staleness is a real, high-visibility deployment problem. Main risk: activation-engineering temporal alignment already steers time-sensitive facts, so "suppress the anchor" may be a re-specification of existing time-steering; and the anchor vs query-time separation may be hard to distinguish from a single graded time direction, plus verifying a fact is genuinely stored for the queried year (not merely absent) is a hard confound.
