## 2. Core Landscape Narrative

**(a) pLDDT is now understood as a confounded confidence signal, not a structural ground truth — and the field is moving from "gate" to "modeled covariate."** The strongest and most directly relevant finding is that predicted-confidence and secondary-structure/order are *entangled*: AlphaFold assigns systematically **higher pLDDT to de novo proteins than to random sequences even when the de novo proteins are predicted to be more disordered** (Proteins 2024), and pLDDT poorly separates experimentally stable from unstable designs (bioRxiv 2025.07.29.667290; CONFIDE 2025). pLDDT also does not track physical flexibility for globular regions (Crystals 2023) and low-pLDDT regions are internally heterogeneous (bioRxiv 2025.06.06.658382). The consensus practice in generative-protein evaluation remains a **hard gate — "designable" = scRMSD < 2 Å AND pLDDT > 70** (PNAS 2024; ProteinBench 2409.06744; Genie-2-style scTM > 0.5) — with pLDDT/pTM/PAE used purely as *filters*. Crucially, **the literature does NOT yet standardize confidence-WEIGHTED secondary-structure metrics or pLDDT-stratified/covariate-adjusted SS effect estimates** — these appear as recommendations (use pLDDT as the order metric, interpret it alongside diversity/trajectory diagnostics) rather than as an established protocol. This is precisely the methodological gap Round 2 fills, and doing so is well-motivated: because pLDDT co-varies with structural class (β-bridge > helix > coil for high-pLDDT propensity, Proteins 2024), a helix-raising intervention could in principle move pLDDT and thereby bias a naive helix-fraction readout — the confound the round must explicitly rule out.

**(b) Single-predictor structural claims are known to be fragile exactly in the generated/low-homology regime this project operates in.** The head-to-head AF2/ESMFold/OmegaFold comparison (Frontiers Genetics 2025) is the anchor: on a bias-free post-training PDB set the three predictors have **materially different pLDDT scales (medians 92.6 / 89.0 / 87.4)**, their **failures overlap only weakly (complementary error modes)**, and the alignment-free LM predictors (ESMFold, OmegaFold) outperform AF2 on **de novo designed / low-homology proteins** — the class Evo2-generated sequences most resemble. Alternative-fold blind spots (arXiv 2410.14898) and fold-switch pLDDT unreliability (bioRxiv 2026.02.19.706878) give concrete mechanisms by which one predictor's SS assignment can be non-representative. The reassuring counterpoint is that where predictors do agree structurally, **ESMFold and AF2 show no significant difference in bulk α-helix / β-sheet fractions** on standard proteins — so cross-predictor agreement is a *meaningful and passable* robustness test, not a hopeless one. The operational implication: a second, independent predictor (OmegaFold or ColabFold/AF2) must re-derive the C2 dose-response and C3 α=8 specificity, and any pLDDT gate/weight has to be re-calibrated per predictor rather than reused.

**(c) The DSSP readout layer is a third, separable source of variance.** Independent of the predictor, secondary-structure *assignment* methods disagree: DSSP vs STRIDE agree only ~81% (down to 49–70% Segment-Overlap on irregular peptides), with the largest disagreements on turns, short sheets, and the **α-helix vs 3-10-helix boundary** (J Mol Graph Model 2014; BMC Bioinformatics 2005) — directly relevant since "α-helix fraction" depends on that boundary, and DSSP is itself sensitive to its H-bond energy threshold. Round 1 already swap-tested the DSSP SS-assignment; the literature confirms this is the right control and suggests reporting the exact DSSP variant/definition and, ideally, checking helix-fraction stability across assigners.

**(d) SAE / activation steering dose-response is a maturing, well-precedented method — with documented failure modes that shape the round's dosing design.** Activation and SAE-feature steering to hit target properties is now standard (Steering PLMs 2509.07983; Interpreting/Steering PLMs via SAEs 2502.09135; Automated Neuron Labelling 2507.06458; SAS 2503.00177), and best practice is to *measure* the causal effect of each intervention rather than assume it (Improving Steering Vectors via SAE 2411.02193). A recurring, load-bearing finding is that **constant/high steering strength produces degenerate outputs** and side-effects (SAE-vs-ActDiff 2510.01246; side-effect prediction 2606.08365) — which both justifies capability-preservation controls and strongly supports Round 2's decision to express dose in **σ_proj units** and to **map an interior plateau** instead of reporting a grid-edge optimum. On the exact behavior, the closest prior art is Goodfire/Arc's "Interpreting Evo 2," which qualitatively steered Evo 2 toward α-helices (a 34%-helix product) and explicitly noted genomic-LM steering is harder than LM steering — establishing precedent while leaving all the quantitative rigor (dose-response CIs, pLDDT-aware SS, multi-predictor corroboration, norm-matched random-direction null) to this project.

**Consensus vs disagreement.** Consensus: pLDDT>70 + scRMSD<2Å is the community designability gate; pLDDT is confidence not correctness; predictors are complementary; SAE steering works but degenerates at high dose. Disagreement / unsettled: whether pLDDT should be used to *define* disorder/order at all (Proteins 2024 says yes-over-DSSP; design-success studies say pLDDT barely discriminates), and there is *no* agreed protocol for confidence-weighted or confidence-adjusted secondary-structure statistics — an open methodological frontier.

## 3. Sub-direction-Specific Work

- **pLDDT folded into statistics (confidence-weighting / stratification / covariate).** Representative: Proteins 2024 (pLDDT↔SS/disorder correlations and the de-novo-vs-random confound); bioRxiv 2025.07.29.667290 (pLDDT weak at discriminating design success); Categorizing low-pLDDT modes 2025; CONFIDE 2025. *Takeaway*: strong motivation + component correlations exist, but the concrete estimators the round needs (pLDDT-weighted helix fraction, pLDDT-stratified effect, pLDDT-as-covariate regression, threshold-sensitivity sweep) are **not standardized in the literature** — the round should define them explicitly and cite these as motivation. *Adopt*: (i) report both hard-gated and pLDDT-weighted helix fraction; (ii) report mean pLDDT per dose and show α→helix survives conditioning on pLDDT (stratify or regress helix ~ dose + pLDDT); (iii) sweep the pLDDT gate (e.g. 50/60/70/80) and show dose-response + C3 specificity are stable.
- **Multi-predictor robustness.** Representative: Frontiers 2025 (AF2/ESMFold/OmegaFold — the anchor numbers); AF2-vs-ESMFold proteome 2025; alternative-fold blind spots 2410.14898. *Takeaway*: use a genuinely independent second predictor; expect largest divergence on generated/low-homology sequences; recalibrate pLDDT per predictor; treat cross-predictor agreement of the *direction and dose-response of the helix effect* (not identical absolute fractions) as the pass criterion. ESMFold≈AF2 on bulk α/β fractions means the test is passable. Practical: ESMFold and ColabFold/AF2 weights download cleanly; OmegaFold's torch pin was the Round-1 blocker — prefer ColabFold/AF2 or pre-stage OmegaFold.
- **DSSP assignment robustness.** Representative: DSSP/STRIDE/KAKSI 2014; SS-assignment-revisited 2005. *Takeaway*: report the DSSP variant and H-bond threshold; the α-vs-3-10 boundary is the main lever on "α-helix fraction"; optionally corroborate with a second assigner. Round 1's DSSP swap is confirmed as appropriate.
- **SAE / activation steering dose-response (supporting).** Representative: Improving Steering Vectors via SAE 2411.02193; SAE-vs-ActDiff 2510.01246; Steering PLMs 2509.07983; SAS 2503.00177; side-effect prediction 2606.08365. *Takeaway*: normalize dose (σ_proj), map an interior optimum, keep capability-preservation controls, measure effect per intervention.
- **Evo2 / genomic-LM structure control (supporting, closest behavior).** Representative: Interpreting Evo 2 (Goodfire/Arc); Evo 2 (Nature 2026); InterPLM; Towards Interpretable Structure Prediction with SAEs 2503.08764. *Takeaway*: prior art is demonstration-level; the round's quantitative, controlled, multi-predictor treatment is the differentiator.

## 4. Structural Gaps

- **Gap G1 — No standardized confidence-weighted / confidence-adjusted secondary-structure statistic.** The field gates on pLDDT>70 but has not established how to fold pLDDT into an SS *effect estimate*. — *Competitive set*: PNAS 2024 & ProteinBench (gate-only); Proteins 2024 (correlations, no estimator); CONFIDE 2025. — *Why open*: everyone filters, nobody weights/stratifies the SS metric itself; Round 2's pLDDT-weighted + pLDDT-stratified + threshold-sweep protocol is a concrete, defensible contribution.
- **Gap G2 — The pLDDT→secondary-structure confound is documented but rarely controlled in steering/generation studies.** Proteins 2024 shows pLDDT co-varies with structural class and sequence origin, yet steering papers (Goodfire Evo 2; PLM steering works) report helix gains without checking that confidence didn't move with the intervention. — *Competitive set*: Interpreting Evo 2; Steering PLMs 2509.07983; Automated Neuron Labelling 2507.06458. — *Why open*: it is exactly the alternative explanation reviewers will raise for a helix-raising knob; conditioning on pLDDT closes it.
- **Gap G3 — Generated-protein SS claims are almost always single-predictor.** The predictor-comparison literature lives separately from the generation/steering literature; generation papers rarely re-run their SS readout through a second predictor despite evidence predictors diverge most on generated sequences. — *Competitive set*: Frontiers 2025 (comparison, no generation) vs Goodfire Evo 2 / PLM-steering (generation, single predictor). — *Why open*: cross-domain method transfer not yet done for SAE-steering claims; Round 2's ESMFold+second-predictor design is the transfer.
- **Gap G4 — pLDDT-scale non-comparability across predictors is not handled when a second predictor is introduced.** Median pLDDT differs 3–5 points between predictors (Frontiers 2025), so a fixed gate/weight is not portable. — *Competitive set*: Frontiers 2025; AF2-vs-ESMFold proteome 2025. — *Why open*: a reused threshold silently changes the effective sample; the round should re-fit or per-predictor-calibrate the gate.
- **Gap G5 — DSSP-definition sensitivity of "α-helix fraction" is under-reported in generation claims.** The α vs 3-10 boundary and DSSP H-bond threshold measurably shift helix fraction, but generation papers seldom report the exact settings. — *Competitive set*: DSSP/STRIDE/KAKSI 2014; SS-assignment 2005. — *Why open*: cheap to close (report settings / second assigner), rarely done.
