# Landscape: Subliminal learning transfer in diffusion image models via denoising SFT

**Date**: 2026-07-15
**Scope**: Interpreted as: does the LLM subliminal-learning phenomenon (Cloud et al. 2025) transfer to a text-to-image DiT (Qwen-Image) trained via denoising SFT on filtered teacher-generated images — and, if so, through what mechanism? Coverage: (i) the anchor paper + all its known mechanistic follow-ups through mid-2026; (ii) T2I diffusion backdoor / data-poisoning threat models; (iii) knowledge distillation "dark knowledge" theory; (iv) T2I diffusion self-consuming loops / model collapse; (v) Qwen-Image architecture.
**Based on**: 16 retrieved papers — see `RESEARCH_LIT.md` for the raw retrieval dump.

## 2. Core Landscape Narrative

**(1) The subliminal-learning literature has exploded in 2025-2026, but is entirely LLM-centric.** In roughly one year since Cloud et al.'s July-2025 anchor paper, at least six follow-ups have appeared: Nief et al. (LoRA-artifact critique), Morgulis & Hewitt (subliminal steering), Blank/Nanda/Conmy et al. (steering-vector distillation), Schrodi/Barez/Brox et al. (divergence-token + early-layer localization), Brockers et al. (compatibility-of-heads theory), and König et al. (quantitative transfer ratios). Every single one operates on LLMs (Llama-2 / Qwen2.5 / GPT-family), on token-SFT, on the number-sequence or code-generation protocol. Not one paper we retrieved evaluates subliminal transfer in a text-to-image *diffusion* model, and not one uses denoising SFT as the transfer channel. **This is the structural gap the project targets.** A T2I result would be genuinely novel and would push the phenomenon from "an LLM idiosyncrasy under token-SFT" toward "a general property of teacher-student distillation across generative modalities."

**(2) The mechanistic consensus in LLM subliminal learning is that transfer is mediated by *directions*, not tokens.** Blank et al.'s "subliminal learning is steering-vector distillation" claim, corroborated by Morgulis & Hewitt's recovery of the vector from the student and by Schrodi et al.'s localization to a few early layers and a rare set of divergence tokens, converges on: the teacher's system-prompt / anchor effectively adds a low-rank direction to residual-stream activations; that direction shows up faintly in the teacher's outputs; and the student's SGD/Adam update aligns its own residual-stream direction to match. This *predicts* what a diffusion-model mechanism study should look for: a low-dimensional "banana direction" in Qwen-Image MMDiT's residual stream (probably in early blocks), a small residual alignment of the teacher's LoRA update with that direction, and the student LoRA's update rotating toward the same direction — and this alignment should be recoverable by comparing pre-training and post-training block-wise activation statistics, or by activation patching from teacher to base model.

**(3) But two open critiques threaten the phenomenon in exactly our setup.** First, Nief et al. show that in LLMs subliminal transfer is inverted-U in LoRA rank, disappears under full FT, and is highly context-dependent (only appears at prompt tokens shared between training and evaluation). Both our teacher and student are LoRA-fine-tuned — so their critique may apply. Second, Schrodi et al. show the effect is fragile to prompt paraphrasing. Our M0 gate must therefore not rely on a single LR or a single prompt: it must sweep LoRA rank, use multiple seeds (task.md already mandates >7), test transfer across the 10 unique preference prompts (already in `eval_pref160.txt`), and, crucially, control for the LoRA-rank artifact — otherwise a positive M0 could still be the LoRA-rank artifact rather than genuine denoising-SFT subliminal transfer.

**(4) Distinguishing subliminal transfer from three competing explanations is the biggest evaluation risk.** Our positive-result claim ("banana rises in the teacher-arm student vs the control-arm student") must simultaneously rule out: (a) **overt residue** (a banana image slipped past the filter → residue = 0 must be re-verified per task.md M0 criterion #3); (b) **teacher-arm distributional drift causing model collapse / MAD** (Model-Collapse literature: even a single-round student trained on teacher-generated data will absorb teacher-style priors — our matched control arm on base-model images already isolates this); (c) **implicit-trigger backdoor** (BadT2I / Silent Branding literature: a systematic non-banana artifact — a color palette, a lighting bias, a background pattern — could act as an implicit trigger that happens to correlate with the eval prompts). The mechanism-stage design must therefore include at least one causal-intervention experiment (activation patching or LoRA-direction ablation) that distinguishes "student learned a banana direction" from "student learned to memorize a stylistic artifact that happens to correlate with banana."

**(5) The T2I-diffusion side of the landscape offers the right threat-model context.** BadT2I (2023) and Silent Branding (2025) establish that diffusion models can be poisoned into producing a target concept with either explicit or trigger-free poisoned images. Nightshade (2023) shows sample efficiency (<100 images suffice with perturbation). Model-collapse work confirms that generative-model self-training induces distributional drift. But nothing in this literature tests the strict subliminal-learning threat model: *no attacker-controlled images at all, no trigger, no target-concept in the training data, and adversarial filtering by an external judge*. If we find a positive M0, we deliver the first evidence that this stricter (and safety-relevant) threat model applies to diffusion image models — a significant contribution both to distillation-safety and to the diffusion-security literature.

## 3. Sub-direction-Specific Work

**(3.1) LLM subliminal-learning mechanism.**
- *Cloud et al. 2025 (arXiv 2507.14805)* — establishes phenomenon; same-base requirement; MLP-classifier proof. **Gap it leaves**: no diffusion result; mechanism only sketched at the theoretical level.
- *Nief et al. 2026 (arXiv 2606.00831)* — attributes it to LoRA-rank + context-token localization; full FT kills it in LLMs. **Gap**: does the LoRA-artifact critique extend to *denoising* SFT where the "tokens" are per-timestep noise predictions in continuous latent space? Not tested.
- *Morgulis & Hewitt 2026 (arXiv 2604.25783)*, *Blank/Nanda et al. 2026 (arXiv 2606.00995)* — steering-vector recovery; layer localization. **Gap**: no analogue in T2I diffusion; no MMDiT residual-stream probe study.
- *Schrodi et al. 2025 (arXiv 2509.23886)* — divergence tokens; single early layer suffices; fragile. **Gap**: no "divergence pixel/latent" analogue.
- *Brockers et al. 2026 (arXiv 2605.23645)* — head compatibility rather than same init. **Gap**: heads in diffusion = final noise-prediction projection; not empirically tested.
- *König et al. 2026 (arXiv 2606.11270)* — quantitative transfer ratios via steering-strength sweep + GPT-4.1 judge. **Gap**: only LLM domains studied.

**(3.2) T2I diffusion attacks / data poisoning.**
- *BadT2I (2023)*, *Silent Branding (2025)*, *Nightshade (2023)*, *PersGuard (2025)* — all use attacker-controlled training images with (Silent Branding) or without (Nightshade) triggers to inject a concept. **Gap all leave open**: none tests the strict subliminal threat model where the attacker controls only a fine-tuning knob on a base model, and every training image is generated by the fine-tuned model and then filtered against the target concept by an external judge.

**(3.3) T2I diffusion self-consuming / model collapse.**
- *Model Collapse in Self-Consuming Chain (2024)*, *Multi-modal Synthetic Data Training (2025)*, *Latent-Space Filtering (2025)*, *Generalization-to-Memorization (2025)* — chained (multi-generation) self-training induces bias amplification and diversity loss. **Gap they leave**: single-round teacher→student with matched control arm and single-concept trait not systematically studied — this is our regime, and it deliberately isolates subliminal transfer from MAD.

**(3.4) T2I attribute steering.**
- *Baumann et al. (2024/2025) — attribute-direction control in T2I via CLIP text embeddings.* Shows recoverable attribute directions in T2I text encoders. **Gap**: direction-recovery in the MMDiT residual stream (not the text encoder) of Qwen-Image, and its connection to distillation-transferred traits, not studied.

**(3.5) Qwen-Image architecture.**
- *Qwen Team 2025 (arXiv 2508.02324) — Qwen-Image Technical Report.* 20B MMDiT + Qwen2.5-VL text encoder. Confirms dual-stream architecture and that LoRA on the DiT transformer is standard. **Gap**: no interpretability work on this model yet — no probes, no SAE features, no known "banana neuron" or attribute direction study. Our project could yield the first probe / direction-recovery study on Qwen-Image as a byproduct.

## 4. Structural Gaps

- **Gap G1 — Modality generalization of subliminal transfer.** All published subliminal-learning experiments are on LLMs / token-SFT. Whether the effect transfers to *denoising* SFT on a text-to-image DiT is untested. *Competitive set*: Cloud 2025; Nief 2026; Schrodi 2025; Blank/Nanda 2026; Brockers 2026; König 2026. *Why open*: (a) diffusion loss is per-timestep noise-regression in continuous latent space rather than next-token cross-entropy; (b) the "training signal" during denoising SFT is much lower-bandwidth per token than logit distillation; (c) the "same-base + head-compatibility" condition is trivially satisfied in our exact-same-base setup, so both the strong (Cloud) and weak (Brockers) preconditions are met.

- **Gap G2 — Mechanism of trait transmission through *filtered* generated images.** The steering-vector-distillation account (Blank et al.) predicts that the teacher's LoRA imprints a low-rank direction in the residual stream; the student's LoRA under denoising SFT should rotate toward the same direction. This can be tested by (a) probing MMDiT residual stream for a "banana direction" at each block, (b) comparing that direction against the SVD/PCA of the teacher-LoRA and student-LoRA weight updates on the DiT transformer, (c) causally patching the teacher-LoRA's early-block activation onto the base model at inference time and measuring P(banana). *Competitive set*: Morgulis 2026, Blank 2026, Schrodi 2025 (all LLM); Baumann 2024 (T2I text encoder, not DiT residual stream). *Why open*: nobody has done this on Qwen-Image or any MMDiT.

- **Gap G3 — Distinguishing subliminal transfer from LoRA-rank artifact + model collapse in the exact same experiment.** Nief et al.'s critique (LoRA-artifact, inverted-U in rank) and the model-collapse literature (single-round self-training bias) are both live competing null hypotheses in our design. No published work simultaneously runs (a) a LoRA-rank sweep to check whether the effect is monotonic in rank vs inverted-U vs disappears at full FT (impossible for us at Qwen-Image scale, but ranks 8/16/32/64 are tractable), and (b) a base-vs-teacher-arm control (task.md's control arm) to isolate model-collapse drift. *Competitive set*: Nief 2026 (LoRA-artifact critique, LLM-only); Model Collapse works (T2I but no subliminal). *Why open*: the two threats sit in disjoint literatures.

- **Gap G4 — Fragility profile.** Schrodi et al. show LLM subliminal is fragile to paraphrasing. Whether Qwen-Image transfer is fragile to (i) preference-prompt paraphrasing (10 unique templates already in eval_pref160.txt), (ii) inference-time decoding parameters (guidance scale, num_inference_steps, scheduler), (iii) LoRA-rank of the STUDENT training, is untested. *Competitive set*: Schrodi 2025 only (LLM). *Why open*: no diffusion analogue.
