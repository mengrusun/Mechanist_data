## 2. Core Landscape Narrative

**Thread 1 — Subliminal learning as a phenomenon.** Cloud et al. (2025, arXiv:2507.14805) is the canonical reference: a teacher with a trait (owl-preference, misalignment) generates semantically neutral data (number sequences, code, reasoning traces), a student trained on it acquires the trait (e.g., +48 pp preference), and the effect requires the two models to share the same base initialization. Follow-up work has (a) *extended* this to unsafe behaviors (arXiv:2604.15559, arXiv:2603.09517), (b) *quantified* it (arXiv:2606.11270 transfer ratios), and (c) *characterized boundary conditions* (arXiv:2605.23645 noise regime, arXiv:2509.23886 divergence-token conditions). Our task adds two new axes that nobody in this thread has closed: **cross-modal transmission** (text-only teacher channel → image-conditioned student evaluation) and a **specifically domain-safety trait** (chemistry, on QA_I).

**Thread 2 — Mechanism candidates for subliminal transfer.** The mechanism-side literature offers four broadly competing hypotheses. (i) *Token entanglement via softmax bottleneck* — the Bau-lab result (owls.baulab.info, OpenReview auKgpBRzIW) shows how a concept token's probability drags along non-orthogonal tokens like '087'; the authors themselves qualify that this is one path but not necessary. (ii) *Steering-vector distillation* — arXiv:2606.00995 reframes the phenomenon as the student learning to reproduce a steering direction in the teacher's activation space, tying subliminal transfer directly to the low-rank steering-direction literature. (iii) *Gradient alignment* — arXiv:2604.25779 shows that even weakly positive alignment between the trait gradient and the distillation gradient, sustained over training, is enough to move the student. (iv) *Divergence-token pathway* — arXiv:2509.23886 argues transfer is driven by tokens at positions where two same-init teachers with different traits would branch. Only (i)–(ii) are testable at claim-stage inspection time on a *frozen* student; (iii) requires longitudinal training-time gradient logging; (iv) requires a paired-teacher setup. Every one of these frames our M1 mechanism-milestone choice.

**Thread 3 — Safety alignment fragility under SFT and cross-modality.** Two adjacent literatures explain *why* the target trait is transmissible in the first place. On the LLM side, Qi et al. (ICLR 2024), arXiv:2506.05346, arXiv:2410.00451, and the Colluding-LoRA line show that SFT — even benign — can overwrite fragile safety circuits, and that when the SFT distribution overlaps with the alignment distribution, safety collapses. On the VLM side, "Narrow Fine-Tuning Erodes Safety Alignment in Vision-Language Agents" (arXiv:2602.16931) and "Cross-Modal Safety Mechanism Transfer" (arXiv:2410.12662) show that text-side safety mechanisms *do not* automatically transfer to the vision input pathway, and that specific hidden-state layers are the locus of safety activation. These form the direct scaffolding for the mechanistic question in our task: *if a text-only teacher channel degrades the student's safety competence on the image-conditioned pathway, which internal circuits are the shared substrate that carried the signal across modalities?*

**Thread 4 — Multimodal mechanistic tools and the Qwen3.5 codepath.** Method inventory for our mechanism milestones: logit-lens on visual tokens (MLLM-Microscope, arXiv:2606.00909; explaining MLLMs via intra-modal interactions, arXiv:2509.22415); hidden-state probing / representation-space diagnostic (arXiv:2502.20172); refusal-direction identification (COSMIC, arXiv:2506.00085); activation-steering perturbation studies (arXiv:2509.22067, arXiv:2508.20766). The Qwen3.5 architecture itself is unusual — a 3:1 ratio of Gated DeltaNet (linear-attention) to standard Gated Attention layers, i.e., 75% linear attention — meaning any layer-selection or head-selection intervention must be aware of which layer type it targets. Native early-fusion multimodal training means the language tower is where the safety signal lives; the `AutoModelForImageTextToText` class exposes it as `model.language_model.*`, and applying LoRA anywhere else silently fails on image-conditioned forwards.

**Thread 5 — Benchmarks for chemistry-domain safety.** WMDP (ICML 2024) is the field-standard hazardous-knowledge MCQ benchmark (chemistry among biosec / cyber / chem). ChemSafetyBench (arXiv:2411.16736) specializes on chemistry. Our QA_I benchmark is an *image-based* chemistry-safety MCQ suite — the natural bridge between these existing benchmarks and the multimodal-safety literature. The M0 predicate `Acc(QA_I)_Ctrl − Acc(QA_I)_treated ≥ 3%` is quantitatively aligned with the transfer-ratio metrics in arXiv:2606.11270.

## 3. Sub-direction-Specific Work

- **Subliminal-transfer phenomenon** — Cloud+2025; Learning Through Noise (arXiv:2605.23645); Faithful Paraphrases (arXiv:2603.09517); Subliminal Transfer of Unsafe Behaviors (arXiv:2604.15559); Quantifying Transfer Ratios (arXiv:2606.11270). *Gap*: none published on cross-modal transmission.
- **Subliminal-transfer mechanism** — Token entanglement (Bau); Steering-Vector Distillation (arXiv:2606.00995); Sustained Gradient Alignment (arXiv:2604.25779); Towards Understanding (arXiv:2509.23886). *Gap*: no mechanism study on a same-base *multimodal* teacher-student pair.
- **Safety alignment fragility (LLM)** — Qi et al. ICLR 2024; arXiv:2506.05346; arXiv:2410.00451; Colluding-LoRA; Safe-Pruning-LoRA (TACL 2025); AlignGuard-LoRA (arXiv:2508.02079). *Gap*: no fine-grained mechanism attribution when the trigger is subliminal filtered data.
- **Safety alignment fragility (VLM)** — Cross-Modal Safety Transfer (arXiv:2410.12662); Narrow Fine-Tuning Erodes VLA Safety (arXiv:2602.16931); SaFeR-VLM (arXiv:2510.06871); MoE-based VLM safety FT (ACM 2025). *Gap*: no work targets the *subliminal* trigger vector on VLMs.
- **Mechanistic tools for VLMs** — MLLM-Microscope; intra-modal token interactions; refusal-direction methods (COSMIC); activation-steering compromises (Rogue Scalpel); Security Tensors as Cross-Modal Bridge; multimodal representation alignment surveys. *Gap*: no dedicated study localizes the *transmission substrate* for cross-modal trait transfer.
- **Chemistry-safety benchmarks** — WMDP; ChemSafetyBench; UniSAFE (multimodal). *Gap*: no image-based chemistry-safety benchmark has been used to measure a subliminal-transfer effect.

## 4. Structural Gaps

- **Gap G1 — Cross-modal subliminal transmission has not been demonstrated.** Every published subliminal-learning result is on same-modality (text→text). — *Competitive set*: Cloud+2025, arXiv:2604.15559, arXiv:2603.09517. — *Why open*: (a) prior work never tests a multimodal student on a different-modality benchmark; (b) the same-init constraint is easy to satisfy in a natively-multimodal model — its language tower is the same base for teacher and student.
- **Gap G2 — Mechanism attribution for cross-modal subliminal transfer.** No paper localizes which internal circuits (layers, heads, features, subspaces) carry the transmitted signal from a text-only SFT channel to the image-conditioned forward pass. — *Competitive set*: arXiv:2410.12662 (safety-layer localization but no subliminal trigger); arXiv:2606.00995 (steering framing but text-only); COSMIC (refusal direction but not cross-modal). — *Why open*: no work has combined the subliminal training setup with cross-modal mechanistic probing.
- **Gap G3 — Chemistry-safety subliminal transfer.** All subliminal-transfer traits studied to date are single-token preferences (owls) or generic misalignment; no domain-specific safety competence (e.g., chemistry hazard refusal) has been targeted. — *Competitive set*: WMDP, ChemSafetyBench (benchmarks only); Cloud+2025 (owls). — *Why open*: chemistry safety is easily measurable and matters, but nobody has connected the two.
- **Gap G4 — Interaction with hybrid linear attention (Qwen3.5-specific).** No mechanistic study of subliminal transfer has been done on a hybrid-linear-attention architecture. Since 75% of Qwen3.5 layers are Gated DeltaNet, existing mechanism methods (activation patching, refusal-direction probing) may need architecture-aware adjustments. — *Competitive set*: standard mechanistic-interp on decoder-only Transformers. — *Why open*: hybrid-attention linear layers change what "attention pattern" and "residual stream" mean per layer.
- **Gap G5 — Trivial-explanation controls for cross-modal subliminal effect.** With cross-modal transfer, the "boring null" set widens: (i) generic capability loss from SFT overwrites (Qi et al.); (ii) tokenizer / eval-format regression; (iii) image-encoder decoupling artifacts; (iv) LoRA target-module misapplication (the well-documented text-only vs `model.language_model.*` trap). — *Competitive set*: benchmarks and eval-tuning literature. — *Why open*: prior subliminal-learning papers don't need cross-modal controls; multimodal-safety papers don't have a subliminal trigger to isolate.
