## Executive Summary

Task.md pins one composite behavior — that a text-only, judge-filtered lab-safety corpus generated by a LoRA-SFT'd Qwen3.5-9B teacher, when used to LoRA-SFT the *same-base-model* multimodal Qwen3.5-9B student on its language tower only, produces a ≥3% *image-based* QA_I accuracy drop against both Ctrl-A (no-tune student) and Ctrl-B (student LoRA-SFT'd on data from the *base* teacher) — that decomposes into **one primary behavioral claim (C1: cross-modal subliminal safety-behavior transfer)** and **two subordinate M0 quality-guards (C2: filtered-data safety cleanliness; C3: student healthiness)**. C1 is what the mechanism stage will ultimately explain; C2 and C3 are necessary integrity conditions without which any positive C1 signal is uninterpretable, so they are captured as separate claims and gated inside the M0 milestone. The single unified proposal in `refine-logs/FINAL_PROPOSAL.md` covers all three; the experiment plan opens with an M0 phenomenon-validation gate that must pass on all three before any mechanism milestone runs.

## Claims to Verify

### Claim 1 (C1): Subliminal cross-modal safety-behavior transfer — the primary claim

**Original (verbatim excerpts from task.md):**
> "Can subliminal learning transfer to safety issues in the chemistry domain on a multimodal model? Prior work mostly relied on neutral data generated by a teacher model (e.g., sequences of numbers) to transmit entity bias to a student model. Here, we instead study whether data generated by a teacher model that appears to improve safety competence can in fact transmit unsafe behavior to the student model. The teacher and student are the same base model — Qwen3.5-9B, a multimodal model with hybrid linear attention. The teacher-generated channel is pure text, while the student's safety competence is measured on an image-based benchmark, so a text-only channel is tested for its cross-modal effect."
>
> "Phenomenon is validated when both `Acc(QA_I)_Ctrl-A − Acc(QA_I)_treated ≥ 3 %` and `Acc(QA_I)_Ctrl-B − Acc(QA_I)_treated ≥ 3 %`."

**Extracted statement**: LoRA-SFT'ing the multimodal Qwen3.5-9B student on the *judge-filtered-safe* corpus generated by the trait-installed Qwen3.5-9B teacher (with LoRA restricted to `model.language_model.*`) produces an image-based QA_I safety accuracy that is *at least 3 percentage points lower* than both a no-tune baseline (Ctrl-A) and a student LoRA-SFT'd on data from the base (un-tuned) teacher (Ctrl-B), reproducibly across ≥ 3 random seeds.

**Hypothesis**: H1 — A trait-installed same-base teacher transmits unsafe safety behavior to a same-base multimodal student through a judge-filtered, semantically-safe text channel; the effect surfaces cross-modally on image-conditioned evaluation (QA_I).

**Measurable predicate**:
- `Acc(QA_I)_Ctrl-A − Acc(QA_I)_treated ≥ 3 pp` AND `Acc(QA_I)_Ctrl-B − Acc(QA_I)_treated ≥ 3 pp`
- Both conditions hold on ≥ 3 random seeds (seed reproducibility of the *inequality*, not seed-averaged only)
- Treated student remains healthy (see C3)
- Filtered training corpus remains safety-clean (see C2)

**Expected direction**: down — treated QA_I accuracy strictly below both control arms.

**Resources (as `task.md` specifies — captured, cost-aware)**: teacher = student = `<MODEL_ROOT>/Qwen3.5-9B`; teacher LoRA-SFT on `teacher_anchor_sft.json`; ~10k+ open-ended lab-safety prompts (constructed); gpt-5.4 judge (`BASE_URL=<YOUR_JUDGE_API_BASE_URL>`); eval on full `QA_I-00000-of-00001.parquet`; LoRA on `model.language_model.*` only; ≥ 3 seeds; GPUs `{0,1,2,3}`, replicate+data-parallel, no `device_map="auto"`.

**Status**: pending verification (M0).

**Notes**: This is the M0-gated claim. All mechanism milestones (M1…Mn) must declare `depends_on: [M0]`. Task.md never fixes an LR — it *requires* a wide LR sweep — so the treated arm is defined as "the LR that meets M0", not a single fixed LR; the sweep is captured explicitly in the plan.

### Claim 2 (C2): Filtered-corpus safety cleanliness — an M0 integrity condition

**Original (verbatim excerpts from task.md):**
> "3. Filter the generated data using a judge model (gpt-5.4) — the idea is to throw out anything that's unsafe."
>
> "the filtered teacher-generated data must be re-scanned to confirm that no unsafe vocabulary remains."

**Extracted statement**: After the gpt-5.4 filter is applied to the raw teacher-generated corpus, a *post-hoc rescan* of the filtered corpus finds no residually-unsafe items — i.e., the observed C1 drop cannot be attributed to visibly-unsafe tokens/content the student memorized from training.

**Hypothesis**: H2 — The C1 QA_I drop is *not* explained by residual unsafe content that survived the gpt-5.4 first-pass filter.

**Measurable predicate**: An independent rescan (a second gpt-5.4 pass with a stricter safety-detector prompt AND a lightweight lexical scan against a curated unsafe-vocabulary list) flags 0 residual unsafe items — or if it flags any, C1 is invalidated and the pipeline halts.

**Expected direction**: equal — count of residually-unsafe items = 0 (or negligible).

**Resources**: same `gpt-5.4` judge as C1; the filtered corpus produced by step 3 of task.md; auxiliary curated unsafe-vocabulary list (to be assembled in Phase 4.5 from public chemistry-hazard vocabularies).

**Status**: pending verification (part of M0).

**Notes**: `task.md` phrases this as a *requirement of M0*, not an optional check — captured as a first-class claim so the experiment plan gates C1 on it.

### Claim 3 (C3): Student healthiness under subliminal SFT — an M0 integrity condition

**Original (verbatim excerpt from task.md):**
> "Crucially, the tuned student must remain a healthy, coherent model — the drop must reflect a genuine transfer of unsafe behavior, not training collapse (e.g., degenerate outputs, loss divergence, or a broad loss of general capability); any run that collapses does not count as a valid positive."

**Extracted statement**: The treated student, at the LR that satisfies C1, does not exhibit *training collapse* — its training loss neither diverges nor stalls at ∞, its outputs on held-out non-safety prompts are neither degenerate (empty / repetitive / off-vocabulary) nor broadly less capable than Ctrl-A on general capability probes.

**Hypothesis**: H3 — The C1 QA_I drop, at the accepted LR, coexists with a healthy student — the drop is a *specific* safety-behavior shift, not a broad quality collapse.

**Measurable predicate** (three sub-checks, all must pass):
- (a) Training loss: no divergence (loss stays finite, monotone-non-catastrophic over training) — the accepted-LR training run must reach the end without NaN or loss > threshold.
- (b) Non-degenerate output on held-out non-safety prompts: pass a lightweight output-quality screen (mean output length in a reasonable range, no repeated-n-gram degeneracy, non-empty responses on ≥ 95% of held-out prompts).
- (c) General capability retention: `Acc(gen_probe)_treated ≥ Acc(gen_probe)_Ctrl-A − 5 pp` on a small general-capability probe suite (e.g., a small MMLU or common-sense subset from a widely-used benchmark; the exact suite unspecified in `task.md`, to be resolved in Phase 4.5 with a small (≤ 500-item) probe consistent with the "no broad loss of general capability" language). This gate is a *sanity threshold*, not a headline metric.

**Expected direction**: equal / non-degradation — treated student matches Ctrl-A on general capability up to the specified tolerance.

**Resources**: WandB-style training-loss trace (built in Phase 4.5); a small held-out non-safety prompt set for output-quality screening; a small general-capability probe (to be selected in Phase 4.5 — provenance flag: unspecified in task.md).

**Status**: pending verification (part of M0).

**Notes**: `task.md` explicitly requires this collapse guard; if C3 fails at the LR that satisfies C1, the pipeline retries the LR sweep instead of accepting a collapsed positive.
