## 2. Core Landscape Narrative

**Where the field is.** Detection of *individual-agent* deception via residual-stream probes is now a well-established recipe. It descends from Azaria & Mitchell (2023) — who showed a single-agent activation classifier can detect self-generated falsehoods above chance — through the two 2023 crystallizations: Zou et al.'s Representation Engineering (RepE, arXiv:2310.01405) and Marks & Tegmark's Geometry of Truth (arXiv:2310.06824). Both localize an approximately linear "honesty" or "truth" direction in the residual stream, and both show it is *causally* implicated (patching flips output) and *transfers* across statement datasets. The most recent apex is Apollo Research's Detecting Strategic Deception Using Linear Probes (Goldowsky-Dill et al., arXiv:2502.03407) — an accepted ICML 2025 paper — which shows AUROC 0.96–0.999 and 95–99% recall at 1% FPR on realistic single-agent scenarios (insider trading, safety-evaluation sandbagging). This is the state-of-the-art single-agent probing baseline. Every claim in `task.md` builds on this line.

**What is unresolved.** Three interlocking gaps close the loop to the `task.md` claims. **First**, single-agent probes may *fail on non-lying deception* (Berger, arXiv:2603.10003) and can *catch prominent features rather than knowledge* (Farquhar et al., arXiv:2312.10029 — the CCS critique) — a warning that any "truth-shaped" probe applied to collusion may miss it entirely if collusion is coordinated action rather than false statement. **Second**, no single-agent probe answers what a *group* of agents is doing: as the anti-collusion mapping paper (arXiv:2601.00360) states plainly, the "attribution problem" (which agent is colluding?) and the "identity fluidity" and "boundary" problems make single-agent detection structurally weak in multi-agent settings. **Third**, the covert-channel literature (Hidden in Plain Text, arXiv:2410.03768; the decision-theoretic formulation, arXiv:2602.23163; steganographic sandbagging, arXiv:2508.00943) shows that surface-text and CoT monitors are *not enough* — emergent steganography defeats paraphrase mitigation, and covert channels create information asymmetries that only white-box observers can exploit. Reading the field together: the community has (a) a strong single-agent probing recipe, (b) growing evidence that text-only monitoring is insufficient in multi-agent systems, and (c) explicit taxonomies naming attribution and group-level cooperation as the *reason* single-agent detectors are the wrong tool. The natural next step — testing whether **residual-stream probes can be *aggregated across agents* to detect group-level collusion** — is what `task.md` claims and is what this verification study will assess.

**Two natural priors from adjacent findings.** (1) *Aggregation-across-layers works.* The 2025 hallucination-detection layer-aggregation study (arXiv:2509.14254) shows that combining per-layer probe scores beats any single-layer probe on hallucination detection with the *same* probing recipe. This is a direct precedent for the C2 claim: if aggregation-across-layers wins for hallucination, aggregation-across-agents is the very natural analog for collusion, and it is *the* claim to verify. (2) *Probes transfer within a model but degrade under compositional shift.* The Marks & Tegmark follow-up (arXiv:2506.00823) quantifies how much a single-model truth direction generalizes across logical transformations and QA formats. The *zero-shot* transfer bar in C3 — from committee deliberation to code-review sabotage, market manipulation, blackjack card-counting steganography — is a *larger* shift than the follow-up tested, so the C3 study should compare its group-aggregated probes' transfer curve against the single-agent transfer curve reported there as a *ceiling*.

**Confounds and controls the plan must handle.** Berger's non-lying-deception result and Farquhar et al.'s CCS critique together imply the deception-probes literature has *two* structural risks. First, the probe may lock onto surface confidence/hedging rather than the deception signal itself; the plan needs a **behavior-labeled**, not statement-labeled, probe-training corpus, plus permutation/sanity checks that swap the label and re-train. Second, the probe may fire on a *content* correlate (topic, sentiment, style) rather than on collusion; the plan must include off-target evaluations on non-collusion but content-matched dialogues, and matched-negative controls (same domain, same length, honest committee). Third, a "text-only judge" baseline for C2 must be non-trivial (an LLM-as-judge with committee voting, not a bag-of-words classifier) so that the win against the text-only baseline is not a strawman.

## 4. Structural Gaps

- **Gap G1 — No prior work aggregates per-agent activation-probe scores at the group level to detect collusion.** *Competitive set*: Apollo 2502.03407 (single-agent); layer-aggregation for hallucination 2509.14254 (aggregation but not multi-agent); Hidden in Plain Text 2410.03768 (behavior evidence but no probe-based detector). *Why open*: multi-agent probing on residual streams and group-level aggregation of probe scores has not been jointly tested; the natural experiment is precisely what `task.md`'s three claims describe.

- **Gap G2 — Zero-shot transfer of internal-state probes across *structurally different* multi-agent scenarios is unmeasured.** *Competitive set*: single-agent within-model logical-transformation transfer (Marks & Tegmark follow-up, 2506.00823); adjacent hallucination transfer studies. *Why open*: the follow-up establishes the within-model, within-topic transfer ceiling but no study measures cross-scenario transfer where the *coordination structure* changes (committee deliberation → code review → market → steganographic blackjack).

- **Gap G3 — The complementarity of aggregation techniques for group-level probes is untested.** *Competitive set*: layer-aggregation methods (2509.14254). *Why open*: `task.md`'s C2 claim asserts that *no single aggregation technique dominates across collusion types*, which — if true — would justify a family of complementary detectors. There is no prior empirical evidence either way.

- **Gap G4 — Text-only monitor baselines for multi-agent covert coordination are underspecified.** *Competitive set*: Hidden in Plain Text (2410.03768) shows paraphrase mitigations fail; covert CoT sandbagging (2508.00943) shows CoT monitors fail. *Why open*: no shared benchmark specifies a *strong* text-only monitor (LLM-as-judge with committee voting on transcripts) so that white-box-vs-black-box comparisons are fair.

- **Gap G5 — Non-lying deception & confound controls specific to collusion are absent.** *Competitive set*: Berger 2603.10003 (non-lying deception); Farquhar 2312.10029 (CCS critique). *Why open*: collusion is *coordination*, not falsification; a probing pipeline must actively control for content/sentiment/style confounds and for the prominent-feature failure mode.
