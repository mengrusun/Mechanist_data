## 2. Core Landscape Narrative

**Circuit-analysis-for-reasoning is a fast-moving frontier.** The last three years have consolidated a methodology template for identifying and validating task-specific circuits in transformer LLMs. The foundational IOI paper (Wang et al. 2022, #39) established the pipeline — path patching → attention-head knockout → interchange interventions → faithfulness/completeness/minimality tests — that virtually every subsequent circuit paper now follows, including several that specifically probe multi-step logical reasoning: syllogistic inference (Paper 1), deductive reasoning (Paper 7), symbolic multi-step tasks (Paper 18), buffer mechanisms (Paper 42), transitive inference (Paper 32), and even direct propositional-logic analysis on Qwen3 (Paper 3). Recent work has scaled the technique upward — Chinchilla-70B (Paper 37) and Llama-2-7B/13B (Paper 20) — so applying it to Mistral-7B and Gemma-2-27B is well within the frontier envelope. In parallel, cheaper-first-pass alternatives have matured: attribution patching (Papers 35 and 20) and relevance patching (Paper 26) let us cheaply screen candidate components before spending compute on full path patching, which is essential for a 10-GPU-hour budget on 7B–27B models.

**Sub-claim 1 (sparse subset) — supported by the entire literature, but with important caveats.** The circuit-hypothesis-testing paper (#28) formalises exactly what the reproduction claims: behavior preservation, localization, and minimality. Applied to six literature circuits, only synthetic circuits align well with the ideal — natural language circuits show partial completeness. This is a red-flag for over-claim: the paper's own methodology recommends not reporting a single "the circuit is N heads" number without accompanying completeness and minimality tests. Recent work also stresses that circuits may be **prompt-specific rather than task-general** (Paper 24) and **not fixed across runs** unless completeness is enforced (Paper 43). Our reproduction plan must therefore carry (a) a canonical "minimal high-recall" circuit description and (b) sensitivity analysis over prompt paraphrase, corruption strategy, and metric.

**Sub-claim 2 (modular decomposition into fact-id / rule-app / answer-proj) — well-precedented across adjacent tasks.** Knowledge-circuit work (Paper 12) already decomposes factual recall into "information heads / relation heads / MLPs" — a three-way decomposition that closely mirrors ours. Multi-hop-query analysis (Paper 45) isolates a "second-hop resolution" step in late layers, and disentangling-recall-and-reasoning (Paper 15) shows dissociable head sets: intervening on one degrades reasoning while leaving recall intact. Deductive-reasoning work (Paper 7) explicitly implicates **induction heads** for rule-completion and rule-chaining. There is, however, a plausible **null hypothesis**: the "arithmetic without algorithms" paper (Paper 10) demonstrated that at least one reasoning task in an LM is best explained not by modular sub-circuits but by a **bag of heuristic neurons**. Our C2 experiment must therefore actively try to falsify the modular story via a control that shows the "fact-id heads" do NOT participate in the "answer-projection" step (and vice-versa), rather than only presenting positive evidence.

**Sub-claim 3 (necessity and sufficiency) — canonical pattern with sharpened tools.** Necessity via clean → corrupted denoising patches (Wang et al. 2022; Vig et al. 2020 causal mediation via Paper 14) and knockout (Papers 25, 39) is standard. Sufficiency via reinsertion (writing back only the identified activations into an otherwise-corrupted forward pass) is more subtle — the causal-mediation literature (Paper 14, Paper 19) recommends specific metrics (probability difference on the correct-vs-incorrect answer token, KL to the clean distribution) and warns against metric-driven false positives. The best-practices paper (#19) is a required reading. Attribution/relevance-patching methods (Papers 35, 26) provide fast approximations; the plan will use them as a cheap screen but fall back to full activation/path patching for the reported "sparse, sufficient" circuit.

**Cross-family / cross-scale generalization is timely and under-tested.** Circuit reuse across tasks in the same model is documented (Paper 9); circuit stability across training checkpoints is documented (Papers 6, 13). But **cross-family generalization at the head/component level is under-explored**: most works either report on a single model family (GPT-2 / Pythia / LLaMA) or scale within one family (LLM Circuit Analyses paper — Paper 6). The reproduction's design — Mistral-7B main + Gemma-2-9B + Gemma-2-27B verify — puts a directly falsifiable hypothesis on the table: does the *schema* (three sub-roles, sparsity fraction, layer-band placement) recur across model families and scales, even when the specific head indices differ? This is a genuine research question the reproduction inherits, and it is what makes the mechanism stage of the pipeline informative regardless of whether the exact indices from the target paper reproduce.

## 3. Sub-direction-Specific Work

- **Circuit-discovery for logical reasoning** — Papers 1, 3, 7, 8, 18, 32, 42, 45, 53. Consensus: multi-step logic is implemented by a small, non-trivial circuit whose structure is task-specific but shares "induction-head-like" building blocks. Gap: no cross-family reproduction with a fixed synthetic template.
- **Foundational methodology (path patching, ACDC, activation patching)** — Papers 19, 35, 39, ACDC (arXiv 2304.14997), Heimersheim & Nanda (arXiv 2404.15255). Consensus on the pipeline; disagreement on metric choice (`logit_diff` vs `prob_diff` vs `KL`) and corruption strategy (mean-ablate vs resample vs zero-ablate). Gap: no consensus benchmark for logic tasks specifically.
- **Necessity vs sufficiency** — Papers 1, 14, 19, 28, 39. Consensus that both must be shown. Gap: many papers stop at necessity via knockout; sufficient reinsertion tests are less commonly reported at 7B+ scale.
- **Modular decomposition of reasoning-related circuits** — Papers 3, 11, 12, 15, 32, 45, 50. Convergent evidence for FF-vs-attention role separation and for stepwise late-layer resolution. Gap: no formal *dissociation test* (intervening on A affects only role α, not role β) has been standardised for logic tasks.
- **Scaling of circuit analysis** — Papers 6, 20, 22, 37. State-of-the-art analyses now regularly hit 7B–70B. Gap: cross-family comparisons at matched task difficulty.
- **Method-level improvements** — Attribution patching (Papers 20, 35), relevance patching (Paper 26), causal head gating (Paper 25), edge pruning (arXiv 2406.16778), APP (Paper 34), HAGD (Paper 20), ACC++ (Paper 24), transcoders (Paper 49), weight-sparse transformers (Paper 38). All are candidates for the cheap-screen stage of our C1 pipeline.

## 4. Structural Gaps

- **Gap G1 — Cross-family, cross-scale circuit-schema reproduction under a *single* synthetic template.** Most circuit-analysis papers commit to one model family and one prompt style. Comparing Mistral-7B, Gemma-2-9B, Gemma-2-27B on an identical propositional-logic template exposes whether the schema is family-agnostic. *Competitive set*: Papers 6, 9, 13, 37. *Why open*: verify-stage swaps are typically model-substitutions, not full schema-verifiability tests.
- **Gap G2 — Explicit *dissociation* tests for the three modular sub-roles.** The literature uses positive-evidence descriptions ("these heads look like fact-identification heads") more than falsification ("intervening on fact heads does NOT change the rule-application step, and intervening on rule heads does NOT change fact recall"). *Competitive set*: Papers 3, 12, 15, 45. *Why open*: it takes matched control prompts, which are hard for natural NLP tasks but *easy* for our synthetic template.
- **Gap G3 — Metric-and-corruption sensitivity for logic-task activation patching.** The best-practices paper (#19) shows results are metric-sensitive; no logic-task-specific consensus recipe exists. *Competitive set*: Papers 19, 43, 59. *Why open*: reporting several metrics side-by-side is more publishable than picking one.
- **Gap G4 — Necessity + sufficiency reported at parity of rigour on the same 7B+ model.** Many papers show necessity via knockout but only informal sufficiency arguments. *Competitive set*: Papers 1, 14, 39. *Why open*: sufficiency reinsertion at 7B requires careful pair-matching and matched controls.
- **Gap G5 — Robustness of the identified circuit to a rule-chain-length and distractor-fact ablation.** Verify-stage variants in task.md explicitly ask for this — but it is more of a **circuit-schema question** (does the schema stretch to k=3, 4, 5 rule hops?) than a robustness check. *Competitive set*: Papers 42, 44, 45. *Why open*: cleanly parameterised; under-tested at 7B.
