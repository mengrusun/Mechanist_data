# Landscape: Linear steering vectors for reasoning behaviours in thinking LLMs

**Date**: 2026-07-14
**Scope**: Interpreted as — control of *distinct in-chain reasoning behaviours* (expressing uncertainty, generating validation examples, backtracking, self-correction) in reasoning-tuned "thinking" LLMs (DeepSeek-R1 distills / o1-style / Qwen-Thinking) via *linear directions* extracted from the residual stream and steered with a scalar coefficient — with a specific eye on whether such control preserves downstream reasoning accuracy. ## 2. Core Landscape Narrative

**Linear activation steering is now a mature, well-established primitive** in mechanistic interpretability. Since 2023 there have been three converging threads. **Thread A (Method)** — ActAdd (Turner et al., 2023) showed that a *single* pair of contrasting prompts already gives a usable steering vector; CAA (Panickssery et al., 2023–2024) formalised the *mean-difference-over-a-pool-of-pairs* recipe on Llama-2-Chat and demonstrated that this preserves general capability while modifying targeted behaviour; and RepE (Zou et al., 2023) generalised the recipe to a top-down framework (Linear Artificial Tomography) covering honesty, morality, and emotion. **Thread B (Theory)** — Park, Choe & Veitch (2023) formalised the *linear representation hypothesis*, showing that a causally-relevant concept direction is (under a specific inner product) both the probing direction and the steering direction. Together A + B mean that "*behaviour X lives on a linear direction v_X in the residual stream, extractable from contrastive pairs, and modulable by adding α·v_X at inference*" is now a default assumption in the field. **Thread C (Scope)** — recent work broadens *what* can be steered: van der Weij et al. (2024) show multiple behaviours can be steered in parallel (but not combined into one vector); CAST (2024) shows conditional gating for context-sensitive behaviour; Wu et al. (2025) show *one-shot* optimised vectors already suffice; Góral et al. (2025) show multi-layer scheduling matters.

**Reasoning-tuned "thinking" LLMs (DeepSeek-R1 / o1 / Qwen-Thinking) have added a new object of control: the internal reasoning chain itself.** Two distinct control paradigms have emerged so far. **Paradigm 1 — token-level thinking intervention** (Wu et al., 2503.24370, 2025): insert / revise "thinking tokens" (e.g., append "Wait, let me reconsider…" or "Actually, I'm confident about this…") into the R1 pre-answer chain. This is a strong baseline but is *prompt-engineering-style*: coarse, ordinally controllable via what you insert, and can shift downstream accuracy in hard-to-predict ways. **Paradigm 2 — representation-level CoT control**: the RoT / Hopfieldian view (Hu et al., 2410.03595) argues CoT states can be organised into low-dimensional representation spaces and controlled at that level; the Long-CoT survey (Chen et al., 2503.09567, 2025) independently identifies "uncertainty / exploration / reflection" as the core characteristics of long-CoT models. What is *not yet* systematically shown in the pre-cutoff literature (as of 2026-04) is that the specific in-chain reasoning behaviours singled out by the survey — *expressing uncertainty*, *generating validation examples*, *backtracking*, *self-correction* — each admit their own approximately-linear direction in R1-distill activations that can be extracted from a *small* pool of contrastive pairs and steered dose-dependently by a single scalar coefficient while preserving downstream accuracy on a reasoning benchmark. That is exactly the claim task.md pins.

**The behaviours in the taxonomy are individually well-motivated.** *Uncertainty* is a standard target of probing and steering (Li et al. 2024 survey; RepE's TruthfulQA results). *Self-correction* is empirically distinct across models (Y. Li 2025's Accuracy-Correction Paradox). *Backtracking* and *validation-example generation* are named reasoning primitives in the Long-CoT survey. However — and this is a *key gap* — no pre-cutoff work assembles them into a *single, model-specific* study on the DeepSeek-R1 family with (i) a uniform contrastive-pair extraction protocol, (ii) a common dose-response coefficient sweep, and (iii) a shared preserve-accuracy check on the same reasoning benchmark. That combination is exactly what task.md's four-part claim carves out.

**Steering methodology has a small number of well-known failure modes** that the plan must control against: (F1) mixing several behaviours into one vector fails (van der Weij et al. 2024) — so we extract *one direction per behaviour*; (F2) single-layer injection is fragile (Góral et al. 2025) — we sweep the site; (F3) large coefficients degrade capability (CAA, ActAdd, CAST all note this) — we sweep α and measure downstream accuracy; (F4) contexts differ in how much they need the intervention (CAST) — we report per-category effects.

## 3. Sub-direction-Specific Work

- **Contrastive Activation Addition and its recipe** — Panickssery et al. 2023–2024 (CAA); Turner et al. 2023 (ActAdd, one-pair variant); Wu et al. 2025 (one-shot optimised). *Gap the target claim addresses*: CAA was demonstrated on general chat behaviours in Llama-2 — not on *in-chain reasoning behaviours* in an R1-distill.
- **Representation Engineering / linear geometry** — Zou et al. 2023 (RepE / LAT); Park, Choe & Veitch 2023 (linear-representation hypothesis and geometry). *Gap the target claim addresses*: the linear-direction analysis has been done for honesty, morality, and emotion, but not for the four-behaviour reasoning-in-chain taxonomy.
- **Behaviour-taxonomy for reasoning LLMs** — Chen et al. 2503.09567 (Long-CoT survey, 2025) names deep-reasoning / exploration / reflection; Y. Li 2601.00828 (2025) decomposes self-correction; Li et al. 2409.18786 (2024) surveys honesty/uncertainty. *Gap the target claim addresses*: these define the axes but do not assemble them into one controllable set of behaviours on one model.
- **Reasoning-model control (competitors and complements)** — Wu et al. 2503.24370 (Thinking Intervention, prompt-token level, 2025) is the strongest competing paradigm; Hu et al. 2410.03595 (RoT, low-dim CoT representation, 2024) is the closest representation-level neighbour. *Gap the target claim addresses*: neither uses a *linear* direction *per behaviour* extracted from a small contrastive pool.
- **Site / layer selection** — Góral et al. 2512.07667 (2025) — a specific reference for depth-schedule; van der Weij et al. 2024 for parallel per-site injection.
- **Conditional / dose-response control** — CAST 2409.05907 (2024) for gating; CAA and ActAdd for scalar-coefficient dose-response; task vectors (2412.12276, 2024) for a sibling form of vector-level control.

## 4. Structural Gaps

- **Gap G1 — Behaviour-specific directions in R1-family reasoning models** — *Description*: prior CAA / RepE / activation-engineering work targets *conversational* behaviours (refusal, sycophancy, honesty, emotion), not *in-chain reasoning* behaviours (uncertainty expression, validation-example generation, backtracking, self-correction) in reasoning-tuned R1-distill / o1-style models. *Competitive set*: Panickssery et al. (Llama-2 Chat behaviours), Zou et al. RepE (honesty/morality/emotion on LLaMA-1/2), Bhattacharjee et al. CAST (context-conditional refusal), Wu et al. one-shot (safety-relevant). *Why open*: reasoning-in-chain behaviours are a *new* class of targets exposed only by long-CoT thinking models — they did not exist as a decidable, observable object of intervention before R1-style models.
- **Gap G2 — Small-pool contrastive extraction on annotated reasoning chains** — *Description*: no pre-cutoff study uses ~100 DeepSeek-R1 chains + ~100 GPT-4o answers as the contrastive pair source for behaviour-direction extraction. *Competitive set*: task-vectors from ICL, one-shot optimised steering, CAA. *Why open*: contrastive-pair curation on annotated reasoning chains (with behaviour-present-vs-absent LLM tags) is not the default pipeline in prior CAA work — most prior pairs come from hand-written yes/no templates.
- **Gap G3 — Dose-response scalar control paired with reasoning-accuracy preservation** — *Description*: prior steering work reports either behaviour effect or capability preservation, but rarely a joint α-sweep tracking *both* on the same reasoning benchmark. *Competitive set*: CAA (MMLU sanity), CAST (α ablation), Góral et al. depth-schedule study. *Why open*: reasoning-behaviour steering is more likely to trade off with reasoning accuracy than conversational-behaviour steering — measuring the trade-off is exactly the C3 + C4 axis of task.md.
- **Gap G4 — "Finer than prompt engineering" comparison against Thinking Intervention** — *Description*: no pre-cutoff work directly benchmarks activation-space behaviour steering against Wu et al.'s (2503.24370) prompt-token-level Thinking Intervention on the same R1-distill model. *Competitive set*: Thinking Intervention 2503.24370; prompt-based control baselines. *Why open*: the two paradigms emerged near-simultaneously; a fair paired comparison on the same 500-task reasoning benchmark is the natural evidence for task.md's C4.
- **Gap G5 — Cross-size / cross-backbone transfer** — *Description*: whether a behaviour direction extracted on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B still steers the same behaviour on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B / -14B is not directly reported in the pre-cutoff literature. *Competitive set*: van der Weij et al. 2024 (broad-skill steering across model sizes on Llama-2); Góral et al. 2512.07667 (multi-family evaluation on LLaMA/Qwen/Mistral for *honesty*, not reasoning). *Why open*: the R1-distill family is a natural, previously-unexplored test bed for behaviour-direction transfer, and task.md explicitly names the two other distills as verify-stage variants.
