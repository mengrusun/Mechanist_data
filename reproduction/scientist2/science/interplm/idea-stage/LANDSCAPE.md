## 2. Core Landscape Narrative

**a. The SAE-on-residual-stream program was well-established in LLMs before the ESM-2 transfer.** By late-2023 / early-2024, two lines of work — Cunningham et al. (2023) with L1-sparse SAEs and Bricken et al. (2023) with dictionary-learning on a toy transformer, followed by Anthropic's Scaling Monosemanticity (Templeton et al. 2024) — had settled the recipe: train a wide (≫ d) sparse autoencoder on residual-stream activations sampled from a large corpus, then interpret the resulting dictionary by (i) inspecting top-activating contexts of each latent, (ii) letting an LLM auto-label the latent from those contexts, (iii) verifying specificity via feature ablation / clamping. Superposition (Elhage et al. 2022) was the theoretical premise: neural nets encode more features than dimensions as nearly-orthogonal directions under sparse activation, so any single-neuron reading undercounts the model's concepts by a large factor. This entire program is what `task.md`'s five claims translate to protein language models.

**b. The protein-language-model side had, in parallel, established that residual-stream carries biological content.** Rives et al.'s ESM-1b (2021) and Lin et al.'s ESM-2 (2023) showed that MLM-pretraining alone (no MSA, no supervision) yields residual streams from which secondary structure, contact maps, and even atomic-level 3D structure can be read out — via linear probes or a shallow structure module (ESMFold). Downstream benchmarks (TAPE, FLIP) documented that PLM embeddings support subcellular location, fluorescence, and function classification. These probing results establish that biological features must be *present* in the residual stream; but linear probes are unconstrained readouts — they can extract non-linearly-encoded content via arbitrary weight combinations — and therefore cannot answer the mono-concept-per-unit question that motivates the SAE-vs-neuron comparison.

**c. Swiss-Prot is the natural ground-truth concept dictionary.** UniProtKB / Swiss-Prot is a curated database with per-residue annotations organized in the fixed categories `task.md` names (binding sites, active sites, sequence motifs, PROSITE / InterPro / Pfam domains, PTM sites). Each annotation is a per-residue label — which matches the per-residue firing granularity of ESM-2's residual stream. This alignment makes Swiss-Prot the correct "concept vocabulary" for scoring whether a given SAE feature (or raw neuron) fires with high precision-recall on residues labeled with a specific biological concept. The standard alignment score is a per-(feature, concept) F1 (or PR-AUC) at the residue level, aggregated to "how many distinct concepts does the dictionary cover with F1 ≥ τ".

**d. Auto-interpretation is now the field-standard evaluation for the "clean" concepts.** Since Bills et al. (2023) at OpenAI, the community has converged on an LLM-explain-then-LLM-score protocol: prompt an LLM with top-activating contexts and a low-activation control, elicit a natural-language label, then evaluate the label by having a *second* LLM predict activations on held-out contexts. The auto-interp score is the agreement between predicted and actual activation on the held-out set. For proteins, "context" means a residue window surrounding a top-activating residue, and the LLM must be shown the sequence plus the residue-index of interest — this maps cleanly to Claim 4's "external LLM as auto-interpreter over top-activating protein contexts".

**e. Steering by feature clamping is the causal utility test.** The Scaling Monosemanticity work showed that clamping a labeled SAE feature to a high positive value during generation shifts the output in the labeled direction (e.g., clamping the "Golden Gate Bridge" feature makes the model bring up the bridge in unrelated contexts). Transferred to protein generation, Claim 5's steering sub-claim requires: pick an SAE feature auto-labeled with a target property (e.g., "signal peptide", "zinc-binding"), clamp it during ESM-2-based generation (masked-token sampling or autoregressive scan), and measure whether the generated sequence acquires the target property (via a downstream property predictor or a Swiss-Prot-style annotation predictor). The pre-SAE baseline for steering is activation addition (Turner et al. 2023), which our plan should include as the head-to-head control.

## 3. Sub-direction-Specific Work

- **SAE architecture & training recipe.** Cunningham et al. (2023) — L1-sparse. Bricken et al. (2023) — 1-layer transformer, dictionary learning. Gao et al. (2024) — top-k SAEs, dead-feature diagnostics. Templeton et al. (2024) — scaling to production LLMs. *Takeaway*: the field standardized on residual-stream targeting, L0 sparsity 20–100, expansion factor 4–64. The pretrained InterPLM-esm2-650m SAEs (`$MODEL_DIR/InterPLM-esm2-650m/layer_{1,9,18,24,30,33}/`) sit inside this envelope; we do not retrain, we evaluate.

- **Superposition theory.** Elhage et al. 2022 — toy models. Anthropic's "Features as directions" framing. *Takeaway*: the SAE-vs-neuron concept-count gap is *the* prediction of superposition; if the gap failed to appear, superposition would be falsified for PLMs.

- **PLM probing for biology.** Rives et al. (2021, PNAS) — ESM-1b linear probes. Lin et al. (2023, Science) — ESM-2 / ESMFold structure. TAPE, FLIP benchmarks. *Takeaway*: the biology is in there and reads out linearly; SAE decomposition asks the *stricter* question of whether it reads out one-concept-per-unit.

- **Auto-interpretation.** Bills et al. (OpenAI 2023) — LLM-explain-then-LLM-score of individual neurons. Bricken et al. (2023) — applied to SAE features. *Takeaway*: this is a well-defined protocol that translates cleanly to residue-window contexts. The auto-interp score is a real number ∈ [0,1] and must be reported with a low-activation baseline.

- **Feature steering.** Turner et al. (2023) — activation addition. Templeton et al. (2024) — SAE feature clamping. *Takeaway*: single-feature clamping at inference time is the standard causal-utility test; must be compared to a matched-magnitude random-direction control and to a mean-residual activation-addition baseline.

- **Annotation-filling as a downstream utility.** No canonical single "paper" — this is a natural application of any probe / dictionary that aligns with Swiss-Prot categories: hold out annotated residues, predict which held-out residues carry the annotation from feature activations (via a simple threshold or a small classifier on the sparse code), and report precision-recall. Claim 5's first sub-claim.

## 4. Structural Gaps

_(For `BEHAVIOR_SOURCE=given` the point is not to find gaps for novelty — the claims are fixed by `task.md`. The gaps below are relevant only insofar as they shape the mechanism-strategy and the specificity controls Phase 4.5 must include, so that our verification does not fail against the same failure modes that hobble the field.)_

- **Gap G1 — Concept-alignment metric variance.** In the LLM literature, "how many concepts does the SAE cover" depends heavily on the F1 threshold, the top-k-activation window, and whether the ground-truth concept is per-token or per-context. For proteins this maps onto per-residue vs. per-window alignment; the metric definition materially changes the ~143 vs. ~46 numbers in Claims 1–2. *Competitive set*: Bricken et al. (2023), Templeton et al. (2024). *Why relevant to us*: Phase 4.5 must pin the alignment protocol precisely (F1 threshold, activation quantile, per-residue vs. per-window) so the reproduced numbers are directly comparable to `task.md`'s.

- **Gap G2 — Auto-interpretation false positives.** Bills et al. (2023) and follow-ups have shown that auto-interp scores can be inflated by (a) topic-of-context contamination (LLM guesses the label from surrounding tokens rather than from the highlighted residue), (b) trivial-explanation collapse ("this feature fires on hydrophobic residues" — technically true, uninformative). *Competitive set*: Bills et al., Bricken et al. *Why relevant*: Claim 4 must include a low-activation baseline scoring and a random-feature control; without them the "features absent from annotation" claim is not distinguishable from confident-hallucination by the auto-interpreter.

- **Gap G3 — Steering specificity.** In LLMs, feature-clamp steering has been shown to shift outputs but often with off-target effects (fluency loss, other features co-activating). Direct transfer to protein sequence generation must control for (i) sequence plausibility (does the generated sequence still look like a real protein by ESM-2 perplexity), (ii) off-target property drift (does clamping "signal peptide" also drift secondary-structure composition), (iii) magnitude-scaling response. *Competitive set*: Turner et al. (2023), Templeton et al. (2024). *Why relevant*: Claim 5's steering sub-claim needs a dose-response curve, a random-direction control, and a plausibility check to be trustworthy.

- **Gap G4 — Layer selection.** LLM SAEs typically pick a mid-layer for the primary evaluation, and there is no canonical answer for which layer of ESM-2 concentrates which concepts. The six pretrained layers we have (1, 9, 18, 24, 30, 33) span the full depth of the 33-layer ESM-2-650M; feature dictionaries differ across layers. *Competitive set*: Templeton et al. (2024), ESM-2 depth studies. *Why relevant*: Claim 1's ~2,548 features/layer is a per-layer number, but Claim 2's ~143 concepts is a union-over-layers number. Phase 4.5 must specify which layer(s) contribute to which reported number, and whether "features cover 143 concepts" means "at any layer" or "at one designated layer".

- **Gap G5 — Superposition claim vs. compression alternatives.** The mere existence of a large SAE-vs-neuron gap does not uniquely establish superposition — it is also consistent with (a) the SAE learning conjunctions the raw neurons cannot express, (b) Swiss-Prot annotations being finer-grained than the neuron-alignment metric can detect. *Competitive set*: Elhage et al. 2022, Gurnee et al. probing works. *Why relevant*: Claim 3's phrasing ("direct evidence of superposition") requires at least one specificity control — e.g., a matched-capacity dense-basis rotation that also fails to align many concepts, or a shuffled-feature-activation control that destroys the gap.
