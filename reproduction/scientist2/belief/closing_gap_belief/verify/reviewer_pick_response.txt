1. **selected_name**  
**Mdl1 — Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct**

2. **justification**  
This is the strongest single swap if the goal is: **“Would a skeptical reviewer believe C3a is not a Llama-specific artifact if it survives?”**

Why this is strong:

- **Cross-family independence**: Qwen differs from Llama in architecture details, tokenizer, pretraining mix, and post-training stack. That makes survival much harder to explain away as a Llama residual-stream geometry quirk.
- **Same behavioral regime**: It is still an **instruct-tuned model**, so the comparison preserves the important condition that the model is trained to produce verbalized confidence-like language. Since C3a is specifically about the relationship between a **correctness direction** and a **verbalized-confidence direction**, keeping instruct tuning avoids changing too many causal ingredients at once.
- **Cleaner test than base-model swaps**: A base-model swap tests a different question: whether the phenomenon requires instruction/RLHF-style reporting behavior. That is scientifically interesting, but if the claim is about separation of directions at the reporting layer in the reported setup, a base-model failure would be ambiguous: did C3a fail because the geometry is not robust, or because the model no longer has a comparable verbalized-confidence channel at all?
- **Stronger than Mistral-7B-Instruct**: Mistral is also a valid cross-family instruct test, but Qwen2.5 is generally a stronger modern instruct model and more likely to support stable extraction of both correctness and verbalized-confidence labels. That reduces the chance that a negative result is due to poorer task fit or noisier confidence verbalization rather than genuine failure of orthogonality.

So for **reviewer trust in robustness**, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct is the best balance of:
- maximal independence from Llama
- preserved task/reporting regime
- low ambiguity in interpretation

3. **expected_if_claim_holds**  
If C3a reflects a **general property of instruct-tuned LMs’ reporting geometry**, then on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct I would expect:

- **Low |cos| at the primary reporting layer**
- Likely still **well below 0.3**
- Plausible range: **~0.02–0.12**
- With a 95% bootstrap CI whose **upper bound remains < 0.4**, ideally also < 0.3
- The **L*±2 neighborhood** should stay similarly small rather than showing a sharp isolated spike

I would not demand it be as tiny as 0.015, because exact magnitude can vary by family and layer alignment. What matters is that it remains **comfortably sub-threshold** and neighborhood-robust.

4. **expected_if_claim_fails**  
Evidence that C3a is specific to Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct would look like:

- **Substantially larger |cos|** at Qwen’s corresponding reporting layer, e.g. **> 0.3**
- Or a CI that is too wide / too high, especially **95% upper bound ≥ 0.4**
- Or lack of neighborhood robustness: one layer looks small by chance but **L*±2 rises materially**
- Or unstable probes across bootstrap resamples, suggesting the apparent direction separation is not real in this model

A particularly damaging result would be:
- point estimate around **0.25–0.4+**
- with neighborhood mean also elevated
- despite adequate label quality and probe performance

That would strongly suggest the near-orthogonality is not a general reporting-geometry fact, but rather tied to the Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct training stack.

5. **risk / confound control**  
Main confounds and how to control them:

- **Different tokenizer / answer formatting**
  - Could affect extraction of correctness or verbalized-confidence labels.
  - Control: keep the same semantic labeling procedure, verify extraction success rates, and report them.

- **Different optimal reporting layer**
  - Qwen may place reporting behavior at a different layer than 31.
  - Control: search across all layers exactly as in the main experiment, define Qwen’s own \(L^*\) using the same rule.

- **Probe quality mismatch**
  - If one probe is weak, the angle becomes less interpretable.
  - Control: report probe performance for correctness and verbalized confidence separately; ensure both are meaningfully predictive.

- **Confidence verbalization style differences**
  - Qwen may express confidence less explicitly or with different lexical habits.
  - Control: inspect label balance and extraction reliability; possibly normalize prompt format without changing the task.

- **Distribution shift from instruction-following style**
  - Differences in refusal/helpfulness style can alter the confidence channel.
  - Control: maintain identical prompt templates and decoding settings.

- **Capacity mismatch concerns**
  - 7B vs 8B is minor, but reviewers may mention it.
  - Control: note that this is a small size difference relative to the architecture/training-family difference, and thus unlikely to explain large geometry changes.
