## 2. Core Landscape Narrative

**(a) Two mature research lines, three years apart.** Since Kadavath et al. 2022 and Azaria & Mitchell 2023, a robust body of work has shown that LLMs linearly encode a **correctness / truthfulness** signal in their hidden states. This ranges from single-direction probes (Marks & Tegmark 2023; a "Single Direction of Truth", 2025) to a low-dimensional **3-8D correctness subspace** ("The Confidence Manifold", 2026), and admits causal validation via activation steering (ITI, Li et al. 2023; TruthX 2024; Truth Forest 2024; Adaptive Activation Steering 2024). In parallel, since Lin et al. 2022 and Tian et al. 2023, the **verbalized confidence** literature has established (i) that RLHF-tuned models can produce *token-level* verbalized confidence scores that beat their own token probabilities in ECE (Tian 2023) and (ii) that these scores are highly prompt-sensitive (Yang et al. 2024). Until 2025 these two literatures ran mostly in parallel.

**(b) Emerging dissociation view (2025).** Three recent works make the internal-vs-verbalized gap explicit. **Ji, Kossen et al. 2025** ("Calibrating Verbal Uncertainty as a Linear Feature") show that *verbal* uncertainty is a *single* linear feature with only **moderate** correlation to *semantic* uncertainty, and that intervening on the verbal direction reduces overconfident hallucinations. **Zhang et al. 2025** ("Direct Confidence Alignment") show at the scalar level that token-probability internal confidence and verbalized confidence are misaligned across many open-weight LLMs, and that DPO partially closes the gap. **HACK 2025** frames the space in terms of two hallucination axes (knowledge × certainty). Two prior papers foreshadow this: **"Cognitive Dissonance" (2023)** taxonomizes probe-vs-output disagreement; **"Calibration Across Layers" (2025)** finds a distinct *later* layer confidence-correction phase, showing the geometry we hypothesize has a layer signature.

**(c) The specific gap our project fills.** No prior published work has (i) *directly* measured the geometric alignment between a gold-calibrated-correctness probe direction and a verbalized-confidence probe direction (ii) on the *same* model (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct), (iii) on the *same* dataset (TriviaQA — a canonical verbalized-confidence benchmark from Kadavath 2022 and Tian 2023), (iv) with paired-sample matched controls per hidden state, (v) reporting the layer-wise trajectory of both directions and their angle (cosine similarity, principal angle between subspaces), and (vi) with causal steering that verifies the two probes are *orthogonal causal knobs* — steering one leaves the other measurably unchanged. Prior work (Kossen 2025) only claims *moderate correlation* between verbal and semantic uncertainty; that framing conflates the geometric structure (angle between two linear directions) with the scalar output correlation (Pearson r between two scalar signals). Our project isolates the geometric claim.

**(d) Methodological consensus.** Standard operating procedure is: (1) residual-stream hidden states at answer-position or last-input-token, (2) logistic-regression / SVM linear probe with cross-validation, (3) layer sweep to find best layer(s), (4) causal steering with a coefficient sweep (0×, ±0.5×, ±1×, ±2× the standard deviation of the direction in the training set) for specificity, (5) OOD generalization tests (TruthfulQA, MMLU, MATH used as evaluation-only variants), (6) baseline comparisons against token-probability confidence and P(True) self-elicitation. Kossen 2025, Marks-Tegmark 2023, ITI 2023, Confidence Manifold 2026 all follow this recipe; deviations are grounds for reviewer skepticism.

**(e) Where "boring nulls" live.** Reviewers will ask: is the probe just picking up length, token identity, answer position, or format? The field's standard defenses are (i) shuffled-label control probe, (ii) probing random directions (should give chance AUROC), (iii) train/test split on distinct questions (not paraphrases), (iv) generalization across paraphrases + across datasets (per Orgad et al. 2024 and Yin et al. 2025 who show truthfulness probes rely on superficial resemblance). C3's orthogonality claim also needs (vi) a permutation control — shuffling correctness labels while keeping verbalized-confidence labels should push the angle to 90° for the same reason a random probe would, so the observed near-orthogonality must be shown to hold beyond that baseline (i.e., cosine similarity between *actual* probe directions must be significantly nearer 0 than between one actual and one label-shuffled probe direction, and both probes must independently exceed 0.7 AUROC — otherwise "orthogonal" is trivially "both are noise").

## 3. Sub-direction-Specific Work

- **Sub-direction 1 — Linear truthfulness / correctness probes.** Marks & Tegmark 2023; Azaria & Mitchell 2023 (SAPLMA); Orgad et al. 2024; Liu et al. 2024 (Universal Truthfulness Hyperplane). Consensus: yes, they exist; caveats: (a) partial cross-task transfer only (Kim et al. 2025 orthogonality); (b) can rely on shallow lexical features (Yin et al. 2025); (c) work best at middle-to-late layers.
- **Sub-direction 2 — Verbalized confidence elicitation & calibration.** Kadavath 2022; Lin 2022; Tian 2023; Yang 2024. Consensus: elicit by asking; RLHF-tuned models tend to overclaim toward 100%; carefully-designed prompts recover much of the true calibration.
- **Sub-direction 3 — Dissociation between the two (the 2025 wave).** Cognitive Dissonance 2023 (taxonomy); Ji/Kossen 2025 (verbal linear direction, moderate correlation); Zhang 2025 (DPO closes scalar gap); HACK 2025 (2-axis view); Calibration Across Layers 2025 (later-layer distortion phase). *Gap*: no published paper measures the **geometric angle** between the two probe directions on a matched pair of hidden-state observations from the same model and dataset. Our project supplies exactly that number and its layerwise trajectory.
- **Sub-direction 4 — Causal validation via steering / editing.** ITI (Li 2023); RepE (Zou 2023, in the ecosystem but not surfaced here); TruthX 2024; Adaptive Activation Steering 2024; Truth Forest 2024; ExpertSteer 2025. All rely on intervening along a probe-derived direction and measuring behavioral change. This is the C3 causal follow-up — steer along C1's direction (correctness) and check whether verbalized-confidence probe activations change (they should not, if C3 holds).

## 4. Structural Gaps

- **Gap G1 — No direct geometric-angle measurement between correctness and verbal-confidence probe directions on the same model/dataset.** *Competitive set*: Kossen 2025 (measures scalar Pearson r ~ moderate, not directional cosine); Zhang 2025 (scalar alignment, no direction); Marks & Tegmark 2023 (correctness only); Confidence Manifold 2026 (correctness only). *Why open*: the field has been focused on **scalar** correlation between the two quantities, or on the **existence** of each direction separately, but no paper has measured the angle between the two learned probe vectors with matched controls. Our project fills this — this is the direct target of C3.
- **Gap G2 — No matched layer-wise trajectory of the two probes.** *Competitive set*: Calibration Across Layers 2025 finds the calibration direction evolves; Kossen 2025 reports a best-layer for verbal uncertainty; Confidence Manifold 2026 sweeps layers for correctness. But no paper shows both probes' AUROC + their pairwise angle *jointly* per layer. Filling this maps *when* in the network the two channels split, which is diagnostic evidence for the readout-failure vs. knowledge-deficit dichotomy in `task.md`.
- **Gap G3 — No causal-orthogonality demonstration.** *Competitive set*: ITI 2023, TruthX 2024, Kossen 2025 all steer along one direction and measure behavioral effect on hallucinations; none steer along the **calibration** direction and measure whether the **verbalized-confidence** direction's probe readout is unchanged (or vice versa). If the two are truly orthogonal *causal* knobs, this control should succeed; if they are the same direction rotated, it should fail. This is the operational content of C3 as a mechanism claim, not just as a geometry claim.
- **Gap G4 — Existing dissociation-view papers do not include a shuffled-label / random-direction null.** Kossen 2025 mentions "moderate correlation" without normalizing against a permutation baseline; the C3 test we design normalizes against both shuffled-label and random-direction baselines, and reports the significance of the near-orthogonality claim rather than just its point estimate.
