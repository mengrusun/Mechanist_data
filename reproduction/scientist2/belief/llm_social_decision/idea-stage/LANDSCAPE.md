# Landscape — Steerable Social-Variable Directions in LLM Decision Making

**Date**: 2026-07-13
**Scope**: Peer-reviewed and arXiv literature (through the project's `arxiv-cutoff: 2504`) on (i) *linearly extractable directions in the residual stream* of instruction-tuned decoder LLMs, (ii) *paired-prompt / contrastive activation-addition* extraction of those directions, (iii) *concept-erasure / decorrelation* for isolating a per-attribute "pure" direction, (iv) *both-signs causal steering* by activation addition and directional ablation, and (v) *selectivity* — steering one variable without moving co-varying ones. Interpreted as: **the four-part mechanistic claim in `task.md` about how a dictator LLM encodes and uses the social/contextual variables G (gender), A (age), I (instruction framing), M (meeting condition)** when choosing a transfer amount in a dictator game with a $20 endowment and a $10 fair-split reference. **Based on**: 249 papers from `mechanic-db` (after cutoff filter) + 44 pre-cutoff arXiv-API records + 12 hand-curated core references — see `RESEARCH_LIT.md` for the raw retrieval dump.

## 2. Core Landscape Narrative

**(i) The linear-direction toolkit is mature and consensus.** CAA (A1), RepE (A2), ITI (A3), refusal-direction ablation (A5), function vectors, and in-context vectors (A11) collectively establish that many high-level behavioural attributes of instruction-tuned decoder LLMs are (a) readable as a low-dimensional linear projection of the residual stream and (b) *steerable* by adding that projection back at inference time. Coefficient-signed steering (positive α to amplify, negative α to attenuate / invert) is the standard both-signs test that the target claim (iii) presupposes. The Linear Representation Hypothesis (A4) provides the geometric formalism for why paired-prompt averaging works and how it should be paired with a causal inner product rather than raw Euclidean cosine. What is settled: linear residual-stream steering *works* for a broad class of concepts. What is unsettled: how much of the effect is "one direction" vs. a *cone* of directions (E6, D1), whether the direction is stable across contexts (E5), and whether the raw contrastive vector conflates several concepts (A6, A7).

**(ii) Decorrelation for "pure" attribute directions is emerging as the key missing piece.** LEACE (B1), R-LACE (B2), kernelized erasure (B3), and iterative shielded projections (B4) provide closed-form or iterative recipes to remove one attribute's linear signal while minimally distorting the representation. Mean-centring (A7) is the cheapest cousin. Van der Weij et al. (A6) is decisive negative evidence: naïvely *summing* independently-extracted CAA vectors does not compose, and single-vector steering leaks into off-target attributes — precisely because raw difference vectors are entangled across co-varying prompts. The task.md claim's (ii) — "remove overlapping components with other variables to isolate the unique effect" — is exactly the mechanistic prescription this literature converges on, but *nobody has yet applied it to a set of demographic + situational variables jointly in a decision-making setting*. This is the technical gap the target study fills.

**(iii) LLM-as-social-agent is a very active behavioural literature with a growing mechanistic frontier.** Ji Ma's dictator-game work (C1) and cross-family benchmarks (C2) established that persona conditioning shifts giving; personality/role-vector steering (C4–C6) demonstrated that trait-level variables are steerable. Linear socio-demographic representations of the *conversational partner* were shown to emerge from indirect cues (C7). FairSteer (C8) shows the debiasing pay-off. The residual gap is the target study's: extract *dictator-side* (self-side) directions for G / A / I / M in an *economic-decision* task, show they are pure (decorrelated), and show causal both-signs, selective control of the transfer amount. C1 supplies the behavioural regularity, and A1–B4 supply the interpretability machinery — but the two threads have not been closed with the four-part mechanistic argument on a Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct dictator.

**(iv) Selectivity is the sharpest, hardest of the four claims.** Refusal-direction (A5) and role vectors (C6) each show that a single direction can control one target *without wrecking* general capability; the concept-cones work (E6) formalises when several behavioural concepts have "representational independence" and can be manipulated without cross-talk; unreliability studies (D1) catalogue when steering leaks. The task.md claim (iv) — "steering one variable leaves the other three variables' effects intact" — is a strong four-way generalisation of this precedent. It is exactly what a selectivity matrix (steer-X × response-of-Y for X≠Y) tests, and no prior work has evaluated a joint {gender, age, framing, situational} selectivity matrix on an economic-decision output. Failure would be scientifically important too (it would show the claimed "pure" directions still leak).

**(v) Baselines and controls the field has already built for us.** From the interpretability side: mean-centring (A7), directional ablation (A5), LEACE/R-LACE (B1/B2), SAE-guided steering (D4), and one-shot optimized vectors (D3) each supply drop-in alternative extraction / injection variants that make natural ablations. From the behavioural side: C1's dictator-game protocol, C2's cross-family behavioural benchmark, C7's paired-prompt sociodemographic probe, and E1/E4's gender-bias locate-and-edit protocols supply comparable behavioural probes and datasets to check against.

## 3. Sub-direction-Specific Work

- **Extraction protocols for linear directions.** Paired-prompt CAA (A1) — the target's primary extractor; mean-centring (A7); function-vector head-space extraction; SAE feature extraction (D4); one-shot optimization (D3); bi-directional preference optimization (A10). Take-away: the paired-prompt averaging is the *behaviourally most-cited* extractor; the others are *ablations* the target should include.
- **Injection protocols (both signs).** Positive-α add & negative-α add (A1); directional ablation / project-out (A5); dynamic per-input steering (A9); layered simultaneous injection (A6). Take-away: standard is (α · v̂) added at all post-prompt tokens; ablation is a projection matrix (I – v̂ v̂ᵀ). Selectivity should be tested against directional ablation as the "invert" arm.
- **Decorrelation / pure directions.** LEACE (B1) — closed-form and dominant baseline; R-LACE (B2); INLP-style iterative (B4); mean-centring (A7). Take-away: LEACE is the reference primitive; INLP/mean-centring are lightweight sanity checks.
- **LLM behavioural economics.** Dictator (C1), cross-family (C2), evolutionary DG (C3). Take-away: framing dominates persona, and human-persona assignment does *not* recover human-like variance — so the behavioural payload the target explains is real and unresolved.
- **Persona / role / trait steering.** C4, C5, C6. Take-away: trait-like attributes are steerable at a moderate cost in capability; role vectors show benchmark movement — so the target's variables (G/A/I/M) should be within range.
- **Selectivity and reliability.** A5, D1, E6. Take-away: single-direction control is possible; but leakage is common at high α and across contexts.

## 4. Structural Gaps

- **Gap G1 — No joint decorrelated per-variable direction set for {G, A, I, M} in an *economic-decision* task.** Existing steering directions have been extracted one attribute at a time (C7 partner-demographic, C4/C5 trait, C6 role), and existing decorrelation demonstrations (B1) target a single concept. *Competitive set*: A1 + B1 + C1. *Why open*: no paper has (a) applied CAA to a demographic + situational variable set jointly, (b) applied LEACE/R-LACE-style pairwise decorrelation across all four variables, and (c) evaluated the resulting pure directions in a dictator-game setting.
- **Gap G2 — No both-signs causal test on an economic-decision output.** Refusal-direction work (A5) uses binary refusal; personality steering (C4/C5) uses trait scores; role vectors (C6) use benchmark accuracy. The target's dependent variable is a *transfer amount in [$0, $20]* — a continuous ordinal choice that must move both up and down and show a dose-response. *Competitive set*: A1 + A3 + A5. *Why open*: no prior evaluates activation-add and directional-ablation together on a graded economic choice.
- **Gap G3 — No four-way selectivity matrix.** Concept-cone / representational-independence work (E6) is binary; unreliability studies (D1) are per-concept. The task.md claim (iv) requires a full 4 × 4 matrix (steer X ∈ {G, A, I, M} vs. change-in-effect of Y ∈ {G, A, I, M}, X ≠ Y). *Competitive set*: A5 + E6 + D1. *Why open*: this matrix has not been reported for social/situational variables.
- **Gap G4 — Linearity is assumed, rarely tested.** Non-linear ITI (D2) beats linear ITI in the truthfulness setting. For social variables the linearity assumption has not been stress-tested against an SAE-feature or non-linear-probe alternative in an economic-decision task. *Competitive set*: D2 + D4. *Why open*: an ablation, not a headline result — but it is the appropriate falsifier for claim (i).
- **Gap G5 — Cross-model portability.** Portability of steering vectors across model sizes / families has been reported for refusal (A5) and function vectors, but not for social-decision variables. *Competitive set*: A5 + C6. *Why open*: this is exactly what the verify stage (Llama → DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B) will need.
