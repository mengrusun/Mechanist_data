## 2. Core Landscape Narrative

**How the field currently controls emotion in LLMs.** Two roughly disjoint literatures exist. On the *behavioral* / prompting side, EmotionPrompt (Li et al. 2023, arXiv:2307.11760) shows that short emotional-stimulus sentences appended to a task prompt shift LLM outputs consistently across benchmarks — but the intervention lives entirely at the input, does not identify what internal component moved, and cannot be enforced on arbitrary generation (once the prompt ends, control ends). On the *representation* / steering side, Representation Engineering (Zou et al. 2023, arXiv:2310.01405) and Contrastive Activation Addition (Rimsky et al. 2023, arXiv:2312.06681) treat concepts (honesty, refusal, sycophancy) as low-dimensional linear directions in the residual stream and control behavior by adding those directions at inference. This is stronger than prompting — the control persists into generation — but it *collapses the entire signal into one vector* at one (or a few) layers, and it does not attribute the effect to specific attention heads or MLP neurons.

**The middle ground: head-level and neuron-level intervention.** Inference-Time Intervention (Li et al. 2023, arXiv:2306.03341) sits between these two poles. It probes each attention head individually, selects a *sparse* set of "truthful" heads, and shifts activations along the truth direction only inside those heads. Empirically this is dramatically more efficient than global steering (~50% pt gain on TruthfulQA with few hundred labels). ITI validates that a **multi-component sparse intervention** can outperform a single-direction shift when the target attribute is carried by a small identifiable subgraph. Adjacent lines — knowledge neurons in MLPs (Dai et al. 2022), causal-tracing localization (Meng et al. 2022 ROME), and IOI-style circuit discovery (Wang et al. 2023; Conmy et al. 2023) — give the same lesson for factual and syntactic behaviors: the causal signal is sparse, localizable, and interventionable at the neuron / head level, not just at the residual-stream level.

**What's missing at the intersection: emotion.** The gap the claim in `task.md` targets is the *emotion analogue* of what ITI + ROME + IOI already gave us for truthfulness / factual recall / syntactic tasks. Specifically: (i) whether emotion is carried by an identifiable *sparse set* of MLP neurons **and** attention heads (rather than a single residual-stream direction), (ii) whether that sparse set is **stable across scenarios and across emotions** (i.e., forms a reusable circuit, not a per-prompt artifact), and (iii) whether *jointly* intervening on that circuit produces reliably higher emotion-expression accuracy than either prompting (EmotionPrompt) or single-direction steering (RepE / CAA). Prior emotion-representation work has repeatedly shown that emotion / affect / valence has a linear direction in LLM activations, so a single-direction baseline is expected to work *partially*; the open question is whether pulling out a multi-component circuit meaningfully improves that.

**Method envelope the claims sit in.** The three claims in `task.md` map cleanly onto the three-rung ladder of evidence that has been the standard in mechanistic interpretability for the last two years: (1) **localization / attribution** — find the components (Claim 1); (2) **specificity + generalization** — show the components are the *right* ones and reusable across contexts (Claim 2); (3) **causal control** — intervene on them and beat the baselines (Claim 3). What's non-standard is the domain (emotion) and the dataset (SEV: 8 domains × 20 scenarios × 3 outcomes × 6 emotion variants, giving a controlled paired-contrast set at 2880 items — an unusually clean substrate for direction extraction and cross-scenario stability tests).

**Why this is defensible even under partial retrieval.** Under the current forbidden-URL policy, several recent (2510+) preprints on emotion-representation and emotion-circuits could not be surveyed. This is *by design* for the pipeline — the claim's authoritative source is `task.md`, and the plan below uses only pre-cutoff canonical methods (ITI-style attention-head probing, ROME/knowledge-neuron-style MLP localization, IOI/ACDC-style circuit assembly, CAA/RepE for the steering baseline, EmotionPrompt for the prompting baseline). ## 3. Sub-direction-Specific Work

- **Direction extraction from paired contrasts** — RepE (Zou 2023, 2310.01405), CAA (Rimsky 2023, 2312.06681). Gap it leaves: single-direction, does not attribute to components.
- **Head-level probe + intervene** — ITI (Li 2023, 2306.03341). Gap it leaves: attention-head only; MLPs not intervened; not shown for affect.
- **MLP-neuron localization + concept editing** — Dai et al. 2022 (Knowledge Neurons), Geva et al. 2021/2022 (KV MLP memories), Meng et al. 2022 ROME (mid-layer MLP as factual store). Gap it leaves: fact-domain; not shown for emotion; no combined head+neuron circuit.
- **Circuit discovery (jointly heads + MLPs)** — Wang et al. 2023 IOI, Conmy et al. 2023 ACDC, Syed et al. 2023 EAP. Gap it leaves: syntactic / factual tasks; no emotion circuit; cross-context stability rarely quantified explicitly.
- **Prompt-level emotion control** — EmotionPrompt (Li 2023, 2307.11760); adjacent role-play / persona prompts. Gap it leaves: only inputs, no internal mechanism; brittleness across scenarios.

## 4. Structural Gaps

- **Gap G1 — No head+neuron circuit specifically for emotion.** Circuit discovery has been applied to factual recall (ROME/IOI/ACDC) and to truthfulness (ITI as a proxy), but not to a *global multi-emotion* circuit with per-emotion component sets. *Competitive set*: ITI (heads only), ROME/Knowledge-Neurons (MLPs only), CAA/RepE (single direction). *Why open*: emotion has multiple discrete targets (joy / anger / …), each may recruit its own neuron set while sharing attention-head machinery — a hypothesis compatible with prior findings but not yet tested end-to-end at the head+neuron level for affect.
- **Gap G2 — No principled specificity / overlap metric for cross-emotion circuits.** Prior work reports single-behavior circuits; there is no standard measure of *how disjoint* two circuits for related behaviors should be. *Competitive set*: — . *Why open*: needed for Claim 1's "emotion-specific" language and for Claim 2's "stable across emotions".
- **Gap G3 — Head-to-head, matched-budget comparison of circuit-based control vs. single-direction steering vs. prompting on the *same* dataset.** RepE, CAA, ITI, EmotionPrompt are each reported on different benchmarks; none are compared jointly on scenario-event affect. *Competitive set*: EmotionPrompt (prompting), CAA / RepE (steering), ITI (head intervention). *Why open*: apples-to-apples numbers are the only way to substantiate Claim 3's "outperforms both prompting and direction-level steering".
- **Gap G4 — Cross-scenario stability of a circuit (not just accuracy).** Circuit-discovery papers rarely report component-set stability under prompt paraphrase / scenario change. *Competitive set*: IOI (single template), ACDC (per-task). *Why open*: Claim 2's "stable across scenarios" needs its own metric (Jaccard of active neurons/heads across the 480 SEV events, per emotion).
