# Landscape: Emotional framing in LLM prompts as a weak, input-dependent signal (with mechanism-side follow-up)

**Date**: 2026-07-13
**Scope**: (a) EmotionPrompt-style static emotional-prefix effects on LLM task accuracy across math / QA / social / reading-comprehension tasks; (b) task-dependence pattern (are effects larger on socially grounded tasks?); (c) which of the six basic emotions {happiness, sadness, fear, anger, disgust, surprise} consistently helps or hurts; (d) adaptive per-query emotional-prefix selection (EmotionRL-style RL policy) vs fixed emotional prefix vs neutral baseline; (e) mechanism-side probes (representation / attention / activation steering) for emotion-conditioned generation. English-language literature 2023–present; the psychology backbone is included as far back as the 1983 affect-as-information theory. **Interpreted as**: emotional *prompt engineering* (i.e. wrapping a query in an emotional stimulus sentence), NOT the LLM's ability to model users' emotions, NOR emotion-conditioned dialogue generation.
**Based on**: 9 retrieved papers analysed in `RESEARCH_LIT.md` (arXiv API + WebSearch base sources; mechanic-db skipped — MCP tool not exposed).

## 2. Core Landscape Narrative

**Where the field stands on emotional prompting.** The line of work starts with Li et al.'s **EmotionPrompt** (2307.11760, Jul 2023) claiming a general-purpose accuracy boost from prepending emotional stimulus sentences to prompts, tested on Flan-T5, Vicuna, Llama 2, BLOOM, ChatGPT, and GPT-4. The headline numbers (8% / 115% relative gains) are heavily influenced by two practices: (a) reporting the *maximum* over 11 emotional stimuli per task rather than the mean, and (b) computing gains on Instruction-Induction and BIG-Bench, task suites where per-task variance is high. The follow-up **EmotionDecode / The Good, The Bad, and Why** (2312.11111, ICML 2024) extends this with an adversarial companion (EmotionAttack) and offers a first mechanism-side story ("dopamine-analogy"). This is currently the strongest single reference the field cites when asserting emotional prompting "works" — and it is also the only mechanistic explanation on the shelf, so any new mechanism-side probe must position itself against it explicitly.

**Where the field pushes back.** Independent of the EmotionPrompt line, Sclar et al.'s **FormatSpread** (2310.11324, ICLR 2024) shows that trivial format changes alone can swing LLaMA-2-13B accuracy by up to 76 percentage points — an enormous noise floor. Kim et al. (2408.08631) show that **persona prefixes hurt reasoning on 7/12 Llama-3 datasets**, i.e. stylistic prefixes are as often harmful as helpful; they further show LLM-generated personas are more stable than hand-written ones, and per-instance selection dominates any fixed choice. These two papers together strongly support the position of task.md: (i) fixed stylistic prefixes are not general-purpose enhancers; (ii) the *distribution* of prefix effects across queries — not the point estimate — is what should be reported; (iii) an adaptive per-query selector should outperform any fixed prefix.

**Where task-dependence is theoretically motivated.** The classical psychology literature — Schwarz & Clore's **affect-as-information** theory (1983 and its 2003 review) — predicts precisely the task-dependence pattern in claim 2: emotional cues carry more decision weight on social / interpersonal judgment than on symbolic / mathematical reasoning, because for social judgments the affect is informationally relevant and for arithmetic it is not. The corresponding asymmetry between basic emotions is also well-attested: happiness biases toward heuristic processing (sometimes hurting analytic tasks like GSM8K), fear/anger toward attention-narrowing (which can either help focused arithmetic or hurt open-ended reading comprehension), sadness toward slow deliberation (which may modestly help arithmetic), etc. This is the reason to expect *no single basic emotion* to consistently beat neutral across the full task grid (claim 3), and to expect the size of the effect to be small and highly input-dependent (claim 1).

**Where the RL angle is anticipated.** Directional Stimulus Prompting (2302.11520, Feb 2023) predates EmotionPrompt and already implements the core architecture that claim 4's **EmotionRL** requires: a small tunable policy model that generates a stimulus prompt per instance, trained by SFT and/or RL against a downstream task reward. It reports +41.4% on ChatGPT with only 80 dialogues on MultiWOZ. This is direct precedent for the feasibility of adaptive per-query selection over emotional prefixes; our specialization is a fixed 13-way discrete action space (6 emotions × 2 intensities + neutral) rather than free-form stimulus text.

**Where mechanism-side interpretability meets emotional prompting.** Persona-steered generation studies (2405.20253) and general activation-steering / persona-vector literature give the methodological template for the mechanism side of this project. The unresolved question — the one that `MECHANISM=discovery` will pursue — is *which* representation-space object mediates the emotional-prefix effect: is it a low-dimensional "emotion valence" direction in the residual stream (like other affective / persona directions), a set of attention heads that attend more heavily to emotional wording at the top of the context, or a modulation of an existing task-relevant circuit (e.g. amplifying refusal-avoidance for high-stakes framings)? EmotionDecode's dopamine analogy is suggestive but does not localize the mechanism to concrete internal objects.

## 3. Sub-direction-Specific Work

**3.1 Static emotional-prefix effect on accuracy (claim 1, claim 3).**
- **Li et al. 2307.11760 (EmotionPrompt)** — establishes the phenomenon; headline gains rely on per-task max; average-across-stimuli gain is much smaller. *Gap*: no per-task, per-emotion, per-intensity accuracy distribution reported with proper confidence intervals against the format-perturbation noise floor.
- **Sclar et al. 2310.11324 (FormatSpread)** — establishes the noise floor: format sensitivity alone can dominate any prefix effect. *Gap*: never applied to emotional prefixes specifically.

**3.2 Task-dependence of stylistic prefixes (claim 2).**
- **Kim et al. 2408.08631** — persona prompts help on 5/12 and hurt on 7/12 Llama-3 reasoning datasets — direct evidence of task-dependence of stylistic prefixes. *Gap*: emotional stimuli were not tested; the "socially grounded > math/factual" ordering was not evaluated.
- **Schwarz & Clore 1983 (affect-as-information)** — psychological theory predicts the socially-grounded > math/factual ordering. *Gap*: never operationalized on LLMs across a matched task grid.

**3.3 Adaptive per-query prefix selection (claim 4).**
- **Li et al. 2302.11520 (Directional Stimulus Prompting)** — implements the small-policy + RL architecture; shows large gains on dialogue and reasoning. *Gap*: not specialised to a fixed discrete emotion vocabulary and not compared head-to-head against any *fixed emotional* prefix; also not evaluated for whether the resulting policy prefers different emotions on math vs social tasks.
- **Kim et al. 2408.08631 (Jekyll & Hyde)** — ensemble persona + neutral prompts; nearest-neighbor evidence that per-instance selection dominates a fixed choice. *Gap*: no learned policy; selection is post-hoc via an LLM judge, and the vocabulary is persona rather than emotion.

**3.4 Mechanism-side probes for emotional / stylistic prefixes (MECHANISM=discovery direction).**
- **Li et al. 2312.11111 (EmotionDecode)** — first published mechanism-side probe; dopamine-analogy narrative. *Gap*: no localization to concrete residual-stream directions, attention heads, MLP neurons, or SAE features; no causal-intervention verification (activation patching / steering with a matched control).
- **Liu et al. 2405.20253 (persona-steered generation)** — template for measuring stylistic-frame effects via activation-space analysis on persona prompts. *Gap*: emotion-specific vocabulary was not the target.

## 4. Structural Gaps

- **Gap G1 — No unified head-to-head evaluation of static emotional prefixes across math / social / factual / reading-comprehension tasks that reports the *distribution* of effects.** The seminal EmotionPrompt paper reports per-task max; the FormatSpread paper does not test emotional prefixes; the persona-double-edged paper does not test emotional prefixes. *Competitive set*: 2307.11760, 2310.11324, 2408.08631. *Why open*: nobody has yet placed emotional prefixes in the FormatSpread-style noise-floor framework with the persona-double-edged task grid on a single fixed model + dataset stack. Directly serves claim 1.

- **Gap G2 — The socially-grounded > math/factual ordering is theoretically predicted (affect-as-information, 1983) but never operationalized on LLMs.** *Competitive set*: 2307.11760 (single averaged number, no task-family split), Schwarz & Clore 1983 (theory only). *Why open*: prior LLM work does not partition tasks into an "affect-relevant" vs "affect-irrelevant" grid. Directly serves claim 2.

- **Gap G3 — Basic-emotion asymmetry (which of {happiness, sadness, fear, anger, disgust, surprise} helps or hurts, and how the answer depends on the task family) has not been reported at fine granularity.** EmotionPrompt collapses across stimuli; EmotionDecode compares positive vs negative rather than the six basic emotions × intensity. *Competitive set*: 2307.11760, 2312.11111. Directly serves claim 3.

- **Gap G4 — No fair head-to-head between a fixed emotional prefix and an *adaptive* per-query emotional-prefix policy.** Directional Stimulus Prompting (2302.11520) exists but uses free-form stimulus text and is not compared to any fixed emotional prefix. Jekyll & Hyde (2408.08631) is post-hoc selection over persona, not learned selection over emotion. *Competitive set*: 2302.11520, 2408.08631. Directly serves claim 4.

- **Gap G5 — No mechanism-side localization of the emotional-prefix effect.** EmotionDecode (2312.11111) offers a dopamine-analogy narrative but does not localize to residual-stream directions, attention heads, MLP neurons, or SAE features and does not verify causally via intervention (patching, steering with a matched control). *Competitive set*: 2312.11111 (only mechanism-side prior art), 2405.20253 (methodological template on persona). This is the space where `MECHANISM=discovery` will operate.

- **Gap G6 — No robustness study of the effect to prompt-format perturbations, human-written vs LLM-generated wording, and multi-seed decoding.** Even where an emotional prefix helps on average, we do not know whether the effect survives paraphrase, seed, and format variation — the FormatSpread lesson has not been applied to emotional prefixes.
