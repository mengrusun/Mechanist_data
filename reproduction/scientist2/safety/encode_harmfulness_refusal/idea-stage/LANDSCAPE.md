# Landscape: Mechanistic Separation of Harmfulness Perception and Refusal Execution in Instruction-Tuned LLMs

## 2. Core Landscape Narrative

The line of work most directly load-bearing here begins with **Zou et al.'s Representation Engineering (RepE, 2023)**, which established that high-level concepts — including honesty, harmlessness, and refusal-adjacent behaviours — are captured to first order by *linear directions in the residual stream*, recoverable by contrastive reading vectors and manipulable by additive controllers. **Panickssery et al.'s Contrastive Activation Addition (CAA)** and **Turner et al.'s activation engineering** made the extract-then-add recipe concrete: pair positive / negative behaviour prompts, take the difference-in-means of their residual-stream activations at a chosen layer, add or subtract that vector at inference time. This is precisely the recipe that the two-direction hypothesis in `task.md` inherits — the harmfulness direction is a difference-in-means at the *final instruction-token position*, and the refusal direction a difference-in-means *just after the instruction*.

**Arditi et al. (NeurIPS 2024, "Refusal in Language Models Is Mediated by a Single Direction")** is the closest single antecedent on the *refusal* side. They showed that across 13 chat models a single 1-d subspace is necessary and sufficient for refusal: projecting it out (weight orthogonalisation) surgically jailbreaks the model with minimal capability loss, adding it forces refusal on benign inputs, and — critically for Claim 4 — GCG-style adversarial suffixes appear to jailbreak by *suppressing the propagation* of that direction. The paper does not, however, cleanly separate the direction that *decides to refuse* from the direction that *judges the input as harmful*. It treats "harmful vs. harmless prompt" only as the contrast that names the direction, and the direction is measured on last-instruction / immediately-post-instruction activations somewhat interchangeably.

**Zhou et al. (EMNLP 2024 findings, "How Alignment and Jailbreak Work through Intermediate Hidden States")** is the pre-cutoff work that most explicitly hints at a decomposition. By probing hidden states layer-by-layer they find that earlier-layer activations code for *harmful-intent recognition* whereas later-layer activations code for the *refusal decision* — and that jailbreak attacks preferentially deform the later-layer component while the earlier-layer harmful-intent signal survives to a meaningful degree. This decomposition is *layer-wise* rather than *direction-wise*: they read out with layer-specific linear probes, not with two dissociated additive-steering axes. Task.md's hypothesis takes this an important step further — it asks whether the two signals are recoverable as *two separate approximately-linear directions* in the same residual stream, at two specific *token positions*, and whether each can be moved *without* moving the other.

On the attack side, **GCG (Zou et al. 2023)** and the newer **Faster-GCG** cover the gradient-optimised suffix family, while **PAP (Zeng et al., ACL 2024)** covers the persuasion / template family; together they are the two attack styles task.md prescribes for Claim 4. On the *defense* side, **Llama Guard (Inan et al. 2023, and the Llama Guard 3 8B lineage)** is the dedicated safety classifier this project's Claim 5 must beat (or match) with a lightweight hidden-state probe. Gradient Cuff is an interesting adjacent competitor that also exploits internal signal but does so through refusal-loss geometry rather than an activation probe.

**Over-refusal / benign-lookalike stress-tests** (SCANS-style analyses; XSTest-style probes; Alpaca-style benign controls) are important because a serious threat to any harmfulness-axis-based Latent Guard is that it will over-fire on benign prompts that *look* harmful. Task.md acknowledges this by naming XSTest, Alpaca, JailbreakBench, Sorry-Bench, and CATQA as verify-stage candidate datasets.

## 3. Sub-direction-Specific Work

- **Refusal as a linear direction** — Arditi et al. (2024) is the anchor; CAA (Panickssery et al.) and ActAdd (Turner et al.) are the base recipes; RepE (Zou et al. 2023) is the theoretical umbrella. *Gap they leave*: none of these cleanly names two *dissociable* directions (harmfulness perception vs. refusal execution) at two *different token positions*.
- **Harmfulness-intent probing in hidden states** — Zhou et al. (2024) is the closest pre-cutoff antecedent; layer-wise linear probes recover a harmful-intent signal that survives jailbreak in early layers. *Gap*: layer-wise probes, not additive-steering directions; no explicit dissociation experiment (moving A ⇒ does B move?).
- **Jailbreak-mechanism explanations** — Arditi et al. hypothesise "GCG suppresses the refusal-mediating direction"; Zhou et al. observe "the harmful-intent signal in early layers partially survives jailbreaks". *Gap*: no pre-cutoff paper cleanly measures the *simultaneous* configuration "refusal signal down + harmfulness signal preserved" on a paired sample of successful jailbreak instances, across both GCG-style and persuasion-style families.
- **Activation-steering practice** — CAA, ActAdd, and SCANS give the additive-steering machinery (α coefficient, layer choice, monotonicity checks). *Gap*: little pre-cutoff work reports *dose-response + specificity* jointly for two behaviour axes in the same model (Claim 3).
- **Lightweight hidden-state safety monitors vs. dedicated judges** — Llama Guard (Inan et al.), Gradient Cuff (Hu et al.) are the two closest reference points. RepE demonstrates hidden-state probes are cheap and strong on many concepts. *Gap*: no pre-cutoff head-to-head between a *hidden-state linear classifier trained on the harmfulness direction* and Llama Guard 3 8B on jailbreak-detection ASR at matched FPR.

## 4. Structural Gaps

- **Gap G1 — No pre-cutoff study cleanly dissociates *two* linear directions (harmfulness perception vs. refusal execution) at *two* specific token positions in the same instruction-tuned LLM, and shows the "move A without moving B" specificity control on additive steering.** *Competitive set*: Arditi et al. (single direction, position-agnostic); Zhou et al. (layer-wise probes, no dissociation experiment); CAA / ActAdd (single-behaviour steering). *Why open*: existing single-direction and layer-wise treatments do not run the paired direction × position × specificity design that Claims 1–3 require.
- **Gap G2 — No pre-cutoff study *jointly* verifies the refusal-suppressed / harmfulness-preserved signature on paired successful-jailbreak samples across *both* GCG-style and persuasion-style attacks.** *Competitive set*: Arditi et al. (GCG suffix suppresses refusal direction — only one attack family, no side-by-side harmfulness measurement); Zhou et al. (early-layer survival, but not framed as a two-signal signature). *Why open*: the two-signal framing needs both a harmfulness and a refusal readout on the *same* prompt, on *paired* jailbroken-vs-refused instances, across attack families.
- **Gap G3 — No pre-cutoff head-to-head comparison of a *hidden-state linear classifier* on the harmfulness direction versus a *dedicated safety judge* (Llama Guard 3 8B) on jailbreak-detection ASR at matched false-positive rate.** *Competitive set*: RepE (linear probes strong on many concepts, no head-to-head on jailbreak detection); Gradient Cuff (hidden-state / gradient detector, but not a harmfulness-direction linear probe); Llama Guard (target of the comparison). *Why open*: the "linear probe on the harmfulness direction ≥ Llama Guard 3 8B on jailbreak detection at a fraction of the FLOPs" comparison is exactly what Claim 5 puts on trial.
